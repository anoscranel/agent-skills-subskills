# BATNA and leverage

## Standard

The BATNA — the best alternative to a negotiated agreement — is what you will actually do if this negotiation fails. It is the single largest source of negotiating power, because it determines how much you need the deal. A party with a strong alternative can afford to be firm; a party with no alternative must accept almost anything. The practical consequence is that the most effective negotiation work often happens away from the table, improving your alternative before you negotiate.

Leverage is comparative, not absolute. Your power depends on your BATNA relative to the counterparty's need for the deal and their own alternatives. A strong BATNA against a counterparty who needs you badly is real leverage; a strong BATNA against a counterparty with many options is not. Assessing their alternatives matters as much as building your own. And the BATNA is a fact about the world, not a rhetorical device: an inflated or invented alternative collapses the moment it is tested, and with it your credibility.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_alternative_to_a_negotiated_agreement> and <https://www.pon.harvard.edu/>.

## Decision rules

- Define your BATNA as a concrete action you would actually take, not a vague hope.
- Strengthen your BATNA before negotiating; alternatives are built, not found at the table.
- Assess the counterparty's alternatives and how urgently they need the deal.
- Never invent or inflate a BATNA; a bluff about your alternative is your weakest card.
- Walk away when the offer is worse than your BATNA; that is what the BATNA is for.
- Re-evaluate your BATNA as the negotiation and the world change.

## Procedure

1. List every realistic course of action if no agreement is reached.
2. Choose the best of them and develop it into a concrete, executable alternative.
3. Estimate the counterparty's alternatives and their cost of no deal.
4. Compare the two to locate where the leverage actually sits.
5. Invest in improving your alternative before and during the negotiation.
6. Set the reservation point from the BATNA and hold it against pressure.

## Anti-patterns

- Negotiating with no developed alternative and calling it flexibility.
- Bluffing a stronger BATNA than you have.
- Ignoring the counterparty's alternatives while perfecting your own.
- Accepting an offer below your BATNA because of sunk time.
- Treating the BATNA as fixed after the situation has changed.

## Done checklist

- [ ] BATNA strengthened before negotiating.
- [ ] Counterparty alternatives assessed.
- [ ] Relative leverage located honestly.
