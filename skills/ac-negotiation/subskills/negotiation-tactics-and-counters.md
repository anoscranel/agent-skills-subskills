# Negotiation tactics and counters

## Standard

Tactics are the moves people use to gain advantage at the table: anchoring high or low, extreme opening demands, artificial deadlines, silence, nibbling for extras at the end, good-cop-bad-cop, and appeals to a higher authority who is never available. Knowing them serves two purposes: you recognise when they are being used on you, and you can answer them without being drawn into the same game. The goal of a counter is to neutralise the tactic and return to the substance, not to win the exchange of tricks.

The general counter is to name the tactic and reframe onto interests and objective standards. An extreme opening is answered by asking what objective basis supports it and by re-stating your own well-founded position, not by countering with an equally extreme number. An artificial deadline is tested by asking what really happens when it passes. A nibble is answered by returning to the whole package, since a deal agreed in total is not reopened one item at a time. Silence is answered with patience rather than with a concession to fill the gap. The discipline is to respond to the tactic without abandoning your own preparation.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negotiation> and <https://www.pon.harvard.edu/>.

## Decision rules

- Recognise the tactic before responding to its content; naming it often defuses it.
- Answer an extreme opening with the objective basis, not a mirror-image extreme.
- Test an artificial deadline by asking what genuinely happens when it passes.
- Answer a nibble by returning to the whole agreed package.
- Meet silence with patience, not with a concession to end the discomfort.
- Do not adopt pressure tactics against a weaker counterparty; the agreement will not hold.

## Procedure

1. Identify the tactic being used and say what you observe, neutrally.
2. Ask for the objective justification behind the move.
3. Re-state your own position and the interests it serves.
4. Redirect the discussion to objective standards and the whole package.
5. Take a break if the pressure is affecting your judgment.
6. Record the tactic and your response for the post-negotiation review.

## Anti-patterns

- Countering an extreme anchor with an equally extreme counter-anchor.
- Conceding to end silence or to beat an artificial deadline.
- Reopening an agreed package to satisfy a late nibble.
- Answering good-cop-bad-cop by negotiating with the good cop.
- Using the same manipulation you just objected to.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Tactic recognised and named neutrally.
- [ ] Objective basis requested for the move.
- [ ] Own position and interests re-stated.
- [ ] Discussion returned to standards and the package.
- [ ] Break taken when pressure clouded judgment.
- [ ] Tactic and response recorded.
