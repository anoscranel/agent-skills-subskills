# Negotiation preparation

## Standard

Most negotiations are won or lost before the parties sit down. Preparation has four objects: your interests, the other side's likely interests, your reservation point, and the zone where an agreement is possible. Skipping any one of them turns the negotiation into improvisation, and improvisation favours the better-prepared side.

The first distinction is positions versus interests. A position is what someone says they want; an interest is the underlying reason they want it. Positions are often incompatible on their face while the interests beneath them are not. The second object is your reservation point: the least favourable outcome you would accept rather than walk away, derived from your best alternative, not from hope. The third is the zone of possible agreement: the overlap between your reservation point and the counterparty's. If no overlap exists, the honest outcome is no deal, and discovering that early saves both parties time.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negotiation> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zone_of_possible_agreement>.

## Decision rules

- Write down your interests before your opening position; the position serves the interests.
- Distinguish positions from interests explicitly; prepare questions that surface the other side's.
- Set your reservation point from your best alternative, before you hear the first offer.
- Estimate the other side's reservation point to locate the zone of possible agreement.
- Prepare multiple issues, not one, so there is room to trade.
- If no zone of possible agreement exists, plan to walk away rather than to concede past your floor.

## Procedure

1. List your interests, ranked, and the outcomes that would satisfy each.
2. Set your reservation point and the best alternative that justifies it.
3. Research the counterparty's likely interests, constraints, and alternatives.
4. Estimate the zone of possible agreement; note where it is wide or absent.
5. Prepare the issues you can trade and the objective standards you will cite.
6. Write the opening position and the questions that test your assumptions about them.

## Anti-patterns

- Preparing only your opening demand and nothing behind it.
- Setting a reservation point after the first offer has anchored you.
- Assuming the other side's position is their real interest.
- Negotiating over a single issue with nothing to trade.
- Treating no-deal as a failure instead of a legitimate result.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Interests listed and ranked.
- [ ] Reservation point set from the best alternative.
- [ ] Counterparty interests and constraints researched.
- [ ] Zone of possible agreement estimated.
- [ ] Tradable issues and objective standards prepared.
- [ ] Questions to test assumptions written.
