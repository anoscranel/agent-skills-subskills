# Negotiation record and review

## Standard

Negotiation skill compounds only if each negotiation leaves a record the next one can learn from. The record has three parts: the preparation that preceded the deal, the concessions actually made and received, and an honest review afterward. Without it, negotiators repeat the same mistakes and misremember whether their preparation was any good.

The review's central test is the outcome against the alternative, not against the opening demand. A deal is good if it beats your BATNA and serves your interests, not if you talked the other side down from their first number. Comparing the outcome to the BATNA and to the reservation point tells you whether the negotiation added value or whether you should have walked away earlier. The review also separates preparation from outcome: a well-prepared negotiation can still fail because the zone of possible agreement was empty, and a badly prepared one can succeed by luck. Recording both teaches the right lesson. The concession log answers a different question — whether you gave away more than you gained, and on which issues.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negotiation> and <https://www.pon.harvard.edu/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the preparation: interests, reservation point, BATNA, and estimated zone.
- Log every concession made and received, with the issue it traded against.
- Judge the outcome against the BATNA and your interests, not the opening demand.
- Review preparation separately from outcome; luck is not a method.
- Note which tactics appeared and how each was answered.
- Write the lesson that changes the next negotiation's preparation.

## Procedure

1. Save the preparation record before the negotiation begins.
2. Log concessions and their trades as the negotiation proceeds.
3. Compare the final outcome with the BATNA and reservation point.
4. Assess whether the zone of possible agreement was estimated correctly.
5. Review tactics used and the effectiveness of your counters.
6. Record one concrete change to carry into the next negotiation.

## Anti-patterns

- Judging the deal by how far the other side moved from their opening.
- Rewriting the preparation afterward to match the outcome.
- Recording the win but not the concessions that bought it.
- Learning nothing because the outcome happened to be good.
- Keeping no record, so each negotiation starts from zero.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Preparation record saved before negotiating.
- [ ] Concessions logged with their trades.
- [ ] Outcome compared with BATNA and reservation point.
- [ ] Zone estimate checked against reality.
- [ ] Tactics and counters reviewed.
- [ ] One concrete improvement recorded for next time.
