# Publication ethics and transparency

## Standard

Medical publication carries ethical obligations beyond accuracy: honest authorship, full disclosure of conflicts of interest, and transparency about who wrote and funded the work. Good publication practice exists to prevent the distortions that hidden authorship and undisclosed interests introduce into the medical evidence base.

Authorship follows the ICMJE criteria: an author must have made a substantial contribution to the work, participated in drafting or critically revising it, approved the final version, and accepted accountability for it. Guest authorship — naming someone who did none of this — and ghost authorship — hiding a real writer, often a paid medical writer — are both breaches. A professional medical writer's contribution is acknowledged transparently, not concealed.

Conflicts of interest are disclosed. Funding sources, sponsor involvement, and authors' financial and professional relationships are declared so readers can weigh the work accordingly. Good publication practice guidelines describe how sponsored research and medical-writing support should be handled openly; the obligation is disclosure, not concealment.

Primary sources: <https://www.icmje.org/> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_writing>.

## Decision rules

- Authorship meets the ICMJE criteria; no guest or ghost authorship.
- A professional medical writer's contribution is acknowledged, not hidden.
- All funding sources and conflicts of interest are disclosed.
- Sponsor involvement in the work is stated openly.
- Good publication practice is followed for sponsored research.

## Procedure

1. Confirm each named author meets the ICMJE authorship criteria.
2. Acknowledge any professional medical-writing contribution.
3. Collect and disclose all funding sources and conflicts of interest.
4. State sponsor involvement in design, analysis and writing.
5. Check the disclosure is complete and accurate.
6. Record the authorship and disclosure decisions with the document.

## Anti-patterns

- A guest author who made no qualifying contribution.
- A ghost writer whose work is concealed.
- An undisclosed funding source or conflict of interest.
- Sponsor control of content hidden from the reader.
- Disclosure treated as a formality rather than a real statement.

## Done checklist

- [ ] All authors meet ICMJE criteria.
- [ ] Medical-writing contribution acknowledged.
- [ ] Funding and conflicts disclosed.
- [ ] Sponsor involvement stated.
- [ ] Authorship and disclosure decisions recorded.

Retractions and corrections are part of the same transparency duty. If a published document is later found to contain a material error, it is corrected openly and promptly, and a serious integrity problem is escalated rather than quietly edited away. A document that cites retracted work re-checks that citation, because building new content on a retracted finding propagates the original problem into the wider literature.
