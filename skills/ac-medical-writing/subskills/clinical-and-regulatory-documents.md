# Clinical and regulatory documents

## Standard

Clinical and regulatory documents report clinical research to regulators, ethics committees and sponsors: the clinical study report (CSR), the protocol, investigator brochures and regulatory submission components. They are highly structured, data-dense documents where completeness and accuracy to the source data are obligations, not style choices. The clinical study report in particular must present the full methods, results and interpretation of a study, favourable or not.

Structure follows the relevant standard. The International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) publishes the guidelines that define the content and organisation of clinical study reports and submission components; a CSR follows its prescribed structure rather than an invented one. Each section serves a purpose: synopsis, methods, results, discussion, and the tables and appendices that carry the underlying data. Deviating from the expected structure invites regulatory queries.

Accuracy to the source data is the discipline. Every figure, table and statement must match the study's data and analysis, and unfavourable results are reported with the same completeness as favourable ones. Selective reporting of outcomes or suppression of negative findings is a serious integrity breach, not an editorial choice.

Primary sources: <https://www.ich.org/> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_study_report>.

## Decision rules

- Follow the relevant standard structure (ICH guidelines) rather than an invented one.
- Every figure and statement matches the source data and analysis.
- Report unfavourable results with the same completeness as favourable ones.
- Each section serves its defined purpose; no section is padded.
- The document is reviewed against the source data before submission.

## Procedure

1. Identify the document type and the applicable ICH guideline.
2. Assemble the study data, analysis and prior documents.
3. Draft each section to the prescribed structure.
4. Verify every figure, table and claim against the source data.
5. Confirm negative and unfavourable results are fully reported.
6. Route through clinical, statistical and regulatory review.

## Anti-patterns

- An invented document structure where a standard exists.
- A figure that does not match the source data.
- Selective reporting of favourable outcomes only.
- Suppressing a negative finding.
- Skipping source-data verification before submission.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Document type and applicable guideline identified.
- [ ] Prescribed structure followed.
- [ ] Every figure and claim verified against source data.
- [ ] Unfavourable results fully reported.
- [ ] Clinical, statistical and regulatory review completed.

The protocol is the study's own reference point. The report is written against the protocol as approved, and every deviation from it — a changed endpoint, an amended analysis population, an unplanned interim look — is disclosed with its reason and its effect on interpretation. A result produced by a method the protocol did not specify is labelled post hoc, because presenting an unplanned analysis as if it were pre-specified misleads the reviewer about how much the finding should be trusted.
