# Customer success

## Overview

Acquiring a customer is the expensive part; keeping and growing them is where a subscription business makes its money. Customer success is the discipline of making customers achieve the outcomes they bought for, so they stay, expand, and advocate. It is not support (reacting to problems) and not account management (the relationship alone): it is a proactive, outcome-driven motion measured by retention and expansion, not by how many calls were made.

This skill owns the post-sale lifecycle. Its neighbours carve the rest: product-management decides what to build, user-research observes users, sales wins the new logo. Customer-success takes over at the signature and owns value realisation. The honest boundary: it measures customer outcomes, never its own busyness; a customer who renews while getting no value is a churn risk deferred, not a success.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_success> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_retention>.

## When to use

- Onboarding customers so they reach first value quickly.
- Building health scores and catching churn risk early.
- Running QBRs, success plans, and retention playbooks.
- Measuring NRR, GRR, and what drives expansion.

## When NOT to use

- For deciding what to build (use ac-product-management).
- For observing users in research (use ac-user-research).
- For new-logo sales prospecting and closing.
- For reactive support ticket handling.

## Status and honest limits

Customer success improves retention odds; it does not guarantee a renewal. A customer whose needs the product genuinely cannot meet will churn, and the honest motion says so early rather than papering over it. Health scores are models of reality, not reality — a green score can hide a disengaged champion. NRR above 100% can mask logo churn. Nothing here replaces a product customers actually need; success work amplifies a good product, it does not rescue a bad one.
