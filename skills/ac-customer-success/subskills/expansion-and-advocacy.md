# Expansion and advocacy

## Standard

Expansion is revenue growth within the retained base: more seats, more usage, more products, or a higher tier. It is earned by realised value, not extracted by pressure. The right time to expand is when the customer has achieved their initial outcome and a further need is visible — expanding a customer who has not yet realised value reads as extraction and damages the relationship. Net revenue retention above 100% is the signature of a base that expands faster than it churns.

Advocacy is the customer voluntarily selling for you: a reference, a case study, a review, a referral. It follows from genuine outcomes and is asked for at the moment of demonstrated value, not demanded on a schedule. An advocate who did not get value is a liability, not an asset. Expansion and advocacy both rest on the same foundation: the customer measurably better off than before they bought.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_success> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_lifetime_value>.

This is the land-and-expand strategy in practice: land a customer with a focused initial scope, prove value fast, then expand on the strength of that proof. It works only when the landing genuinely delivers value; a rushed land that never reaches its outcome poisons every later expansion conversation. The expansion conversation should feel to the customer like a natural next step, not a new sale.

## Decision rules

- Expand only after the customer has realised their initial outcome.
- Match the expansion to a visible further need, not a quota.
- Track net revenue retention to see expansion net of churn.
- Ask for advocacy at the moment of demonstrated value.
- An advocate must have genuinely realised value first.
- Measure expansion revenue and advocacy outcomes, not asks made.

## Procedure

1. Identify accounts that have realised their initial outcome.
2. Surface the further need that justifies expansion.
3. Propose expansion tied to that need and its value.
4. Track NRR and its expansion, contraction, churn components.
5. Ask successful customers for a reference or case study.
6. Record expansion and advocacy with the value behind them.

## Anti-patterns

- Expanding a customer who has not realised value.
- Expansion driven by vendor quota, not customer need.
- Asking for a reference from a struggling account.
- Counting NRR without reading its components.
- Advocacy treated as an obligation, not earned.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Expansion follows realised initial value.
- [ ] Expansion tied to a visible further need.
- [ ] NRR tracked with its components.
- [ ] Advocacy asked at demonstrated value.
- [ ] Advocates genuinely better off.
- [ ] Expansion and advocacy outcomes recorded.
