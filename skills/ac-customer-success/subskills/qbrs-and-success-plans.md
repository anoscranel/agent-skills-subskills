# QBRs and success plans

## Standard

The success plan is the shared document that makes value realisation concrete: the customer's stated outcomes, the milestones to reach them, the owner on each side, and the measures that prove progress. It is written with the customer, not for them, and it is the spine of the relationship. A success plan that only the vendor reads is a forecast, not a plan.

The QBR is the recurring review that keeps the plan alive. It is not a usage report or a sales call: it is a working session on the customer's outcomes, what has been achieved, what is blocking value, and what happens next. The right attendees are the people who own the outcome on both sides, including the economic buyer where possible. Multi-threading the relationship — several genuine contacts, not one champion — is what protects the account when a single champion leaves.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_success> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_relationship_management>.

Prepare the customer for the QBR, not just yourself. Share the outcome review and the proposed agenda in advance so the customer can bring their own assessment and the right people. A QBR where only the vendor has prepared becomes a presentation; one where both sides have prepared becomes the working session that actually moves the success plan forward.

## Decision rules

- Write the success plan with the customer: outcomes, milestones, owners, measures.
- The QBR reviews outcomes and blockers, not a feature or usage tour.
- Include the economic buyer, not only the day-to-day champion.
- Multi-thread the relationship; one champion is a single point of failure.
- Every QBR ends with decisions, owners, and dates.
- Re-baseline the success plan when the customer's goals change.

## Procedure

1. Co-write the success plan with the customer's stated outcomes.
2. Set milestones, owners, and progress measures.
3. Schedule the QBR cadence with outcome owners.
4. Prepare the QBR around outcomes, blockers, next steps.
5. Multi-thread contacts across functions and seniority.
6. Record decisions and update the success plan.

## Anti-patterns

- A success plan the customer has never seen.
- A QBR that is a product demo or a sales pitch.
- Relying on a single champion.
- No economic-buyer relationship.
- QBRs with no decisions or follow-up owners.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Success plan co-written with outcomes and owners.
- [ ] Milestones and progress measures set.
- [ ] QBR cadence scheduled with outcome owners.
- [ ] Economic buyer engaged.
- [ ] Relationship multi-threaded.
- [ ] Decisions recorded and plan updated.
