# Retention and churn reduction

## Standard

Churn reduction starts with honest analysis. Separate the churn you can influence from the churn you cannot: customers who outgrow the product, lose their budget, or were never a fit will leave regardless, and pretending otherwise wastes the retention budget. For the churn you can influence, find the real reasons by talking to churned customers and reading the signals, not by guessing. Voluntary churn, involuntary churn (failed payment), and expansion contraction are different problems with different fixes.

Retention playbooks turn the analysis into repeatable action: the at-risk trigger, the intervention, the owner, and the follow-up. Match the play to the cause — a customer who never adopted needs onboarding rescue, a champion who left needs re-multi-threading, a value gap needs an honest conversation about fit. Accept that some customers should churn; forcing a misfit customer to stay costs more than it returns.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churn_rate> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_retention>.

A save-offer is a tool, not a strategy. A discount or a free month can buy time to fix a genuine, fixable problem, but used on a customer who simply gets no value it delays the inevitable and trains the base to threaten churn for a discount. Reserve save-offers for cases where the underlying cause is being fixed, and never let them become the default response to every cancellation.

## Decision rules

- Separate churn you can influence from churn you cannot.
- Find real churn reasons from churned customers and signals, not guesses.
- Distinguish voluntary, involuntary, and contraction churn.
- Match each retention play to the actual cause.
- An unfit customer is allowed to churn; do not buy retention at a loss.
- Measure playbook effect on retention, not on activity.

## Procedure

1. Analyse churned accounts by reason, segment, and timing.
2. Separate addressable churn from unavoidable churn.
3. Interview churned customers for the real reasons.
4. Build a playbook per addressable cause with owner and trigger.
5. Intervene early on the at-risk signal.
6. Measure each play's effect on retention and iterate.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating all churn as preventable.
- Guessing churn reasons instead of asking.
- One generic retention play for every cause.
- Discounting to retain a customer who gets no value.
- Measuring retention work by touches, not saved accounts.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Churn analysed by reason, segment, timing.
- [ ] Addressable and unavoidable churn separated.
- [ ] Real reasons gathered from churned customers.
- [ ] Playbook per cause with owner and trigger.
- [ ] Early intervention on at-risk signals.
- [ ] Playbook effect measured on retention.
