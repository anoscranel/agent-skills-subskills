# Customer success framing

## Standard

Customer success is the proactive discipline of making customers achieve the outcome they bought for, so they renew, expand, and advocate. It differs from its neighbours by intent: support reacts to a problem, account management owns a relationship, sales wins a new logo, and customer success owns value realisation after the sale. The motion is proactive — reaching the customer before they drift, not after they complain.

The economics justify the discipline. Retaining a customer is cheaper than acquiring a replacement, and a subscription business's revenue compounds on the retained base. The frame is the customer's outcome, not the vendor's activity: a customer who logs in daily but never reaches their goal is a churn risk, and one who renews while getting no value is a churn risk deferred, not a success. Define success per customer as the measurable result they bought for, then organise the motion around delivering it.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_success> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_relationship_management>.

## Decision rules

- Define success per customer as their measurable outcome, not your activity.
- Distinguish the motion: support reacts, account management relates, sales closes, success realises value.
- Work proactively; reach the customer before drift, not after complaint.
- Justify the function on retention economics and the compounding retained base.
- A renewal without realised value is recorded as a risk, not a win.
- Map the post-sale lifecycle: onboarding, adoption, value, renewal, expansion.

## Procedure

1. State what each customer bought for, as a measurable outcome.
2. Map the lifecycle stages from signature to expansion.
3. Assign ownership of value realisation at each stage.
4. Set the proactive touchpoints that precede drift.
5. Define the metrics: retention, expansion, outcome realisation.
6. Record the motion and what it is measured by.

## Anti-patterns

- Measuring success by calls made and emails sent.
- Treating a quiet customer as a happy customer.
- Confusing customer success with reactive support.
- Counting a valueless renewal as a success.
- No defined outcome for what the customer bought for.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Success defined per customer as a measurable outcome.
- [ ] Motion distinguished from support, account management, sales.
- [ ] Proactive touchpoints set before drift.
- [ ] Lifecycle stages mapped and owned.
- [ ] Metrics measure outcomes, not activity.
- [ ] Valueless renewals flagged as risk.
