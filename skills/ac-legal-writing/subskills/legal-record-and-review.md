# Legal record and review

## Standard

A legal draft is reviewed on two axes the writer must keep separate: the writing (structure, consistency, readability, citation) and the substance (the legal position, which belongs to a qualified lawyer). The writing review is a checklist, not a vibe: defined terms reconciled, obligations complete, cross-references correct, citations verified, boilerplate appropriate, formatting consistent. Version control keeps the drafting history and the changes between drafts visible, so a negotiated change is never lost and a reverted clause is intentional.

Precedent is a starting point, not a template to ship unexamined. A clause copied from a prior deal carries that deal's assumptions, and the review asks whether each clause fits this matter. The review record states what was checked, what changed and why, and what was left for the lawyer. It also states the boundary plainly: this is a writing review, not legal advice, and the substance is the lawyer's call.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_writing> and <https://www.plainlanguage.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Review writing and substance separately; the lawyer owns the substance.
- Run a written checklist: terms, obligations, references, citations, boilerplate.
- Keep version history with the changes between drafts visible.
- Treat precedent as a starting point and re-check every clause against this matter.
- Record what was checked, what changed and why, and what was left for review.
- State in the record that the review is writing craft, not legal advice.

## Procedure

1. Reconcile defined terms against the body in both directions.
2. Check every obligation for actor, action, standard, deadline.
3. Verify cross-references and citations after all edits.
4. Review each precedent clause for fit to this matter.
5. Run the readability pass and the consistency pass.
6. Record checks, changes, open items, and the not-legal-advice boundary.

## Anti-patterns

- A "review" that only reads for typos.
- Precedent shipped with the prior deal's terms intact.
- Cross-references broken by late edits and never re-checked.
- No version history, so a negotiated change is lost.
- The writing review silently becoming a legal opinion.
- Open items left unrecorded for the next reader to rediscover.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Defined terms reconciled both directions.
- [ ] Obligations complete with actor, action, standard, deadline.
- [ ] Cross-references and citations verified post-edit.
- [ ] Precedent clauses re-checked for fit.
- [ ] Readability and consistency passes done.
- [ ] Record lists checks, changes, open items, and the advice boundary.
