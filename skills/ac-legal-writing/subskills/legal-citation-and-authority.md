# Legal citation and authority

## Standard

Legal citation tells the reader exactly what authority supports a proposition and how much it is worth. The conventions are jurisdiction-specific: the Bluebook dominates United States legal citation, OSCOLA is common in the United Kingdom and Commonwealth, and courts often impose their own local rules. The craft is to cite the authority the reader can retrieve, in the form the forum requires, and to signal its weight honestly.

Authority has a hierarchy. Constitutions sit at the top; statutes and regulations carry binding force, and cases interpret and apply them, with a higher court's decision binding the courts below it. Within any system, binding authority in the jurisdiction outranks persuasive authority from elsewhere. A pin cite points the reader to the exact page or paragraph that supports the proposition. Signals — "see", "see also", "cf.", "compare", "contra" — tell the reader how the authority relates to the proposition, and using the wrong signal misstates the support. Every citation must be verified: the case says what the writer claims, at the page cited, and it has not been overruled or distinguished away.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_citation> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluebook>.

## Decision rules

- Cite in the form the forum or jurisdiction requires, named in the record.
- Prefer binding authority in the jurisdiction over persuasive authority.
- Give a pin cite to the exact page or paragraph supporting the proposition.
- Use signals accurately to state how the authority relates to the claim.
- Verify each citation: the proposition, the page, and the current validity.
- Never cite authority you have not read.

## Procedure

1. Identify the required citation system for the forum.
2. Select the strongest binding authority for each proposition.
3. Format the citation in the required form.
4. Add the pin cite to the supporting page or paragraph.
5. Choose the signal that states the true relationship.
6. Verify every citation and its current validity before filing.

## Anti-patterns

- Citing persuasive authority where binding authority exists.
- A citation to a case's first page when the point is deep inside it.
- "See" used as a universal signal regardless of relationship.
- Citations to authority never actually read.
- Ignoring that a relied-on case was overruled or distinguished.
- Mixing citation systems inconsistently within one document.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Required citation system identified and applied.
- [ ] Strongest binding authority cited for each proposition.
- [ ] Pin cites point to the supporting page or paragraph.
- [ ] Signals state the true relationship.
- [ ] Every citation read and verified for content and validity.
- [ ] Citation form consistent throughout.
