# Contract structure and drafting

## Standard

A contract is a set of instructions that strangers must be able to follow without the drafter present. Its architecture is conventional for a reason: parties, recitals, definitions, operative provisions, representations and warranties, covenants, conditions, term and termination, boilerplate, and signature blocks. The operative provisions carry the deal — what each party must do, by when, and what happens if they do not. Boilerplate (governing law, notices, assignment, force majeure, entire agreement) is not filler; it decides how the contract behaves under stress.

The drafting discipline is consistency and completeness. Every defined term is used exactly as defined, and no undefined synonym creeps in. Every obligation names an actor, an action, a standard, and a deadline. Every "what if" the deal can reasonably raise has an answer: breach, delay, non-payment, early exit, dispute. The test is to read the contract as the opposing party's lawyer looking for the gap.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_writing>.

## Decision rules

- Follow the conventional architecture unless a clause has a reason to move.
- Define every term once and use it identically; banish undefined synonyms.
- Give each obligation an actor, action, standard, and deadline.
- Answer the foreseeable "what ifs": breach, delay, exit, dispute.
- Treat boilerplate as load-bearing, not filler.
- Read the draft as the opposing party hunting for the gap.

## Procedure

1. Set out the deal points as obligations before drafting prose.
2. Lay out the architecture and the definitions.
3. Draft operative provisions with actor, action, standard, deadline.
4. Add representations, covenants, conditions, term, and termination.
5. Draft boilerplate deliberately for this deal, not by habit.
6. Stress-read for gaps, inconsistencies, and undefined terms.

## Anti-patterns

- A defined term later used with a different meaning or a different word.
- Obligations with no actor, no standard, or no deadline.
- Boilerplate copied from another deal without checking it fits.
- Recitals that quietly create obligations they were not meant to.
- Cross-references that point at the wrong clause after edits.
- No answer to early termination or dispute resolution.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Architecture follows the conventional order.
- [ ] Every defined term defined once and used consistently.
- [ ] Every obligation has actor, action, standard, deadline.
- [ ] Foreseeable "what ifs" answered.
- [ ] Boilerplate chosen for this deal.
- [ ] Cross-references and definitions verified after edits.
