# Recruiting and screening

## Standard

A usability study is only as good as its participants. They must match the target users on the characteristics that change behaviour: experience level, domain knowledge, the tasks they actually perform, the devices they use. Testing a consumer app on the product team or on professional designers measures experts, not users, and the findings will flatter the design. A screener selects for the behaviours that matter and excludes the conflicts, without revealing the answers that get someone accepted.

The sample-size question has a famous, frequently misread answer. Testing with about five users per distinct user group tends to surface most of the severe usability problems in a design, because problems are found by observing failures, not by counting people. This justifies small iterative tests, not a population estimate, and it does not mean five is always enough: a product with several distinct user groups needs representation from each, and a question about prevalence needs a quantitative method. The honest posture is small frequent tests across the design's life, not one large test at the end.

Primary sources: <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usability_testing>.

## Decision rules

- Recruit for the behaviours and context that change how people use the product.
- Exclude the product team, designers and anyone with a conflict of interest.
- Screen for behaviour without coaching the qualifying answers.
- Use roughly five participants per distinct user group for problem-finding.
- Cover every materially different user group, not just the convenient one.
- Prefer small repeated tests over one large end-of-project test.

## Procedure

1. List the user characteristics that change behaviour on the tasks.
2. Write a screener that selects for those behaviours and excludes conflicts.
3. Recruit from channels that reach real target users.
4. Run small tests per distinct user group.
5. Iterate the design and re-test rather than enlarging a single round.
6. Record who was recruited, excluded and why in the research plan.

## Anti-patterns

- Testing on colleagues, designers or the product team.
- Recruiting only the easiest-to-reach users and calling them representative.
- Treating five users as a prevalence estimate.
- Skipping a distinct user group because it is hard to recruit.
- One large test at the end instead of small tests throughout.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Behaviour-changing characteristics listed.
- [ ] Screener selects for behaviour, excludes conflicts.
- [ ] Real target users recruited.
- [ ] Roughly five per distinct group for problem-finding.
- [ ] Every distinct user group represented.
- [ ] Recruitment and exclusions recorded.
