# Research record and ethics

## Standard

Research with people carries duties to them and to the reader. To the participant: informed consent before any recording, a clear explanation of what is collected and why, the freedom to withdraw without consequence, and confidentiality about who they are. Recordings, notes and any identifying data are stored with restricted access, retained only as long as needed, and anonymised in anything shared. To the reader: a report that makes the evidence usable without exposing the people behind it.

The report is the product of the research. It states the research questions and mode, the methods used, who participated and how they were recruited, the tasks and conditions, and then the findings with their severity and evidence. Recommendations are specific enough to act on and tied to findings, and the limitations are stated in the main body, not buried: what the sample cannot support, which groups were not represented, and what the method could not see. A report that cannot tell its reader what to do next, and what it cannot conclude, has not finished the research.

Primary sources: <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usability_testing>.

## Decision rules

- Obtain informed consent before recording; explain what is collected and why.
- Allow withdrawal without consequence; keep participation confidential.
- Store recordings and identifying data with restricted access and a retention limit.
- Anonymise participants in anything shared.
- Report questions, methods, participants, tasks, findings and limitations.
- Make recommendations specific and tied to findings; state limitations in the body.

## Procedure

1. Brief participants and obtain informed consent before recording.
2. Store recordings and notes with restricted access and a retention limit.
3. Anonymise participants in notes, quotes and the report.
4. Write the report: questions, methods, participants, tasks, findings.
5. Attach severity, evidence and a specific recommendation to each finding.
6. State limitations and what the study cannot conclude in the main body.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording without consent or a way to withdraw.
- Naming or showing identifiable participants in shared material.
- Findings with no method, tasks or participant description.
- Recommendations untraceable to any finding.
- Limitations omitted or hidden in a footnote.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Informed consent obtained before recording.
- [ ] Data stored with restricted access and retention limit.
- [ ] Participants anonymised in shared material.
- [ ] Report states questions, methods, participants and tasks.
- [ ] Findings carry severity, evidence and recommendations.
- [ ] Limitations stated in the main body.
