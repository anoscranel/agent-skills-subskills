# Interviews and personas

## Standard

A user interview explores goals, context and current behaviour; it is not a usability test, because nothing is being evaluated. The craft is to ask about real past behaviour rather than hypothetical futures, to follow concrete incidents rather than general opinions, and to let the participant's actual workflow, workarounds and frustrations surface. "Tell me about the last time you did this" yields evidence; "would you use a feature that did this" yields politeness.

Personas and journey maps are synthesis artifacts built from that evidence, not invented characters. A persona captures a meaningful cluster of goals, behaviours and context so the team can design for someone specific instead of everyone. A journey map lays out the steps a user takes toward a goal, with their actions, touchpoints, thoughts and pain points at each stage. Both go stale the moment they stop being refreshed by real research, and a persona the team cannot tie to observed users is a poster, not a tool.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_(user_experience)> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_journey>.

## Decision rules

- Ask about concrete past incidents, not hypothetical future use.
- Interview to understand goals and context, not to evaluate a design.
- Build personas from observed clusters, never from imagination.
- Anchor each persona and journey stage to the evidence behind it.
- Refresh personas and maps as new research arrives; retire stale ones.
- Keep the number of personas small enough to design against.

## Procedure

1. Recruit participants who match the target users.
2. Interview around real incidents, goals, workarounds and frustrations.
3. Cluster observations into meaningful behaviour and goal patterns.
4. Draft personas from the clusters, citing the evidence for each trait.
5. Map the journey toward a goal with actions, touchpoints and pain points.
6. Review and refresh on a research cadence.

## Anti-patterns

- Asking "would you use this" and recording the yes as demand.
- A persona invented in a workshop with no research behind it.
- Journey maps drawn from the team's assumptions.
- Too many personas to design for.
- Treating a persona as permanent once published.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Interviews grounded in concrete incidents.
- [ ] Personas built from observed clusters.
- [ ] Each trait traceable to evidence.
- [ ] Journey stages mapped with pain points.
- [ ] Refresh cadence scheduled.
- [ ] Persona count kept design-usable.
