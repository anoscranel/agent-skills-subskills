# Research framing

## Standard

User research splits into two modes that answer different questions, and confusing them wastes the study. Generative research explores: who are the users, what are their goals, what do they do today, where do they struggle. It informs what to build and runs early, before a solution is fixed. Evaluative research tests: does this design work, can users complete the task, where do they fail. It runs once something exists to test, from a sketch to production.

The framing discipline picks the mode before the method. A team that runs a usability test when it still does not know who the user is will measure the wrong thing well; a team that runs open interviews when it needs to know whether the checkout works will collect opinions instead of evidence. The research question names the mode, the decision it informs, and the evidence that would change that decision. A question no answer could act on is not worth a study.

Primary sources: <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usability>.

## Decision rules

- Name the mode first: generative to understand, evaluative to test.
- Tie each study to a decision it informs and the evidence that would flip it.
- Match the method to the question: explore with interviews, evaluate with tasks.
- Test the riskiest assumption or the most-used flow first, not the easiest.
- Do not run a study whose result cannot change anything.
- Reuse existing evidence before commissioning new research.

## Procedure

1. Write the decision the research must inform.
2. Classify the question as generative or evaluative.
3. State what evidence would change the decision.
4. Choose the method that produces that evidence.
5. Identify the riskiest assumption or highest-traffic flow to target.
6. Record the question, mode, method and decision in the research plan.

## Anti-patterns

- Usability testing before knowing who the user is.
- Open interviews when the question is whether a flow works.
- Research commissioned to confirm a decision already made.
- A question with no decision attached.
- Re-running a study that existing evidence already answers.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Decision named.
- [ ] Mode classified generative or evaluative.
- [ ] Method matched to the question.
- [ ] Riskiest assumption or top flow targeted.
- [ ] Evidence-that-would-flip stated.
- [ ] Existing evidence reviewed first.
