# Wording and response bias

## Standard

The question is part of the measurement, and its wording can move the answer as much as the underlying construct. A leading question points at the desired answer ("How much do you support the excellent policy?"); a loaded term smuggles in an evaluation; a double-barrelled question asks two things and leaves the respondent unable to answer either cleanly. These are not stylistic flaws — they manufacture a result that reflects the wording, not the population.

Beyond wording, respondents bring response styles. Acquiescence is the tendency to agree regardless of content, so agreement items overstate support. Social desirability bias pushes answers toward what looks acceptable, so sensitive behaviours are under-reported and admirable ones over-reported. Extreme responding is the habit of choosing scale endpoints, while others cluster at the midpoint. These styles vary across respondents and cultures, so a scale can compare groups that are answering the scale itself differently.

The defences are mechanical. Use neutral, balanced wording with both poles of a construct named. Balance the scale so agreement and disagreement are equally available, and consider reversing some items to detect acquiescence. Reduce social desirability pressure with self-administration, assurances of confidentiality, and indirect phrasing for sensitive items. Keep reading level low and terms concrete and singular in meaning.

Primary sources: <https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Questionnaire_construction>.

## Decision rules

- Remove leading, loaded, and double-barrelled items at review.
- Name both poles of the construct; keep the scale balanced.
- Reverse a subset of items to detect acquiescence.
- Use indirect phrasing and confidentiality assurances for sensitive items.
- Keep wording concrete, singular in meaning, and low in reading level.
- Treat wording changes as instrument changes: re-test before fielding.

## Procedure

1. Review every item for leading, loaded, and double-barrelled wording.
2. Rewrite each in neutral, balanced language naming both poles.
3. Add reversed items where acquiescence is a risk.
4. Flag sensitive items; choose indirect phrasing and administration.
5. Check reading level and term ambiguity.
6. Pretest the revised wording and record changes.

## Anti-patterns

- A question that suggests its own answer.
- Agreement-only items inflating measured support.
- Sensitive items asked directly with no confidentiality framing.
- Vague terms ("regularly", "often") left undefined.
- Reusing wording across modes without checking equivalence.

## Done checklist

- [ ] No leading, loaded, or double-barrelled items.
- [ ] Both poles named; scale balanced.
- [ ] Reversed items where acquiescence is a risk.
- [ ] Sensitive items handled indirectly.
- [ ] Wording concrete and unambiguous.
- [ ] Revised wording pretested.
