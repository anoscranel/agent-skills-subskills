# Survey record and reporting

## Standard

A survey result is only as credible as the methods record behind it. The record is what lets a reader judge whether the number can be trusted and how far it generalises. It states the target population and the sampling frame, the design and its probability basis, the mode and fieldwork dates, the exact instrument, the achieved sample, the response rate and its convention, the weighting applied, and the limitations. A survey reported without these is an assertion, not a finding.

The record separates what the design supports from what it does not. A probability sample supports a population estimate with a margin of error; a non-probability sample does not, and the record must say so rather than imply a population result. Measurement limits are named: the construct's operationalisation, known mode effects, and any wording that could have shifted answers. The record names who was not reached and what that means for the claim.

Transparency extends to the instrument and dispositions. The exact question wording and response options are published, because the wording is part of the finding. Dispositions are tabulated so the response rate can be reproduced. Weighting variables and the weighting's effect on precision are reported. The record ends with the limitations stated plainly, not buried.

Primary sources: <https://www.pewresearch.org/our-methods/> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survey_methodology>.

## Decision rules

- Publish population, frame, design, mode, dates, and the exact instrument.
- Tabulate dispositions so the response rate is reproducible, with its convention named.
- Report weighting variables and the variance effect.
- State what the design supports and what it does not.
- Name measurement limits, mode effects, and unreached groups.
- Put limitations in the main record, not a footnote.

## Procedure

1. Record target population, frame, and coverage gap.
2. Record design, probability basis, and achieved sample.
3. Record mode, fieldwork dates, and administration.
4. Publish the exact instrument and response options.
5. Tabulate dispositions; report response rate and weighting.
6. Write the limitations and the design's supported scope.

## Anti-patterns

- A headline number with no methods record.
- A margin of error implied for a non-probability sample.
- The instrument withheld so wording effects cannot be judged.
- A response rate with no dispositions or convention.
- Limitations omitted or buried.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Population, frame, and coverage recorded.
- [ ] Design and probability basis stated.
- [ ] Mode and fieldwork dates recorded.
- [ ] Exact instrument published.
- [ ] Dispositions and response rate reported.
- [ ] Weighting and limitations stated.
