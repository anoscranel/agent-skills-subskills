# Questionnaire design

## Standard

Each item operationalises the construct: it is the question that turns an abstract idea into a measurable response. The mapping must be explicit — for each item, which aspect of the construct it captures. Open questions elicit free text and discover what the designer did not anticipate; closed questions force a choice among fixed options and enable quantification. Most surveys use closed items for measurement and a few open items for discovery.

Response scales carry their own craft. Likert-type scales measure agreement or intensity; the number of points trades granularity against respondent burden, and a midpoint allows a genuine neutral position while its absence forces a lean. Options must be exhaustive and mutually exclusive — every respondent fits exactly one option, so "other" and "don't know" are deliberate design choices, not afterthoughts. Question order matters: earlier items prime later answers, a sensitive item early can depress completion, and a general question asked after a specific one is contaminated by it.

Pretesting is not optional. Cognitive interviewing asks respondents to think aloud as they answer, revealing that the designer's words do not mean to respondents what the designer intended. A pilot run on a small sample exposes confusing items, missing options, and drop-off points before the full fieldwork commits the budget.

Primary sources: <https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Questionnaire>.

## Decision rules

- Map every item to the aspect of the construct it measures; drop items that map to nothing.
- Use closed items for measurement, open items for discovery.
- Make response options exhaustive and mutually exclusive.
- Choose scale points and midpoint deliberately; record the rationale.
- Order from general to specific; place sensitive items late.
- Pretest with cognitive interviews and a pilot before full fieldwork.

## Procedure

1. List the construct's aspects; draft an item for each.
2. Choose open or closed per item, with the measurement goal.
3. Design response options: exhaustive, exclusive, balanced.
4. Set scale length and midpoint policy.
5. Sequence items general-to-specific, sensitive last.
6. Run cognitive interviews and a pilot; revise the instrument.

## Anti-patterns

- Items that measure nothing the construct requires.
- Overlapping or non-exhaustive response options.
- A double-barrelled item asking two things at once.
- A sensitive question first, depressing completion.
- Skipping the pilot to save time.
