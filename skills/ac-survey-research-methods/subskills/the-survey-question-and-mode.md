# The survey question and mode

## Standard

A survey begins with a construct, not a topic. "Customer satisfaction" is a topic; the construct is the specific, measurable thing — overall satisfaction on a defined scale, likelihood to recommend, frequency of a behaviour. The research question is written as the construct to be measured and the population it describes, before any item is drafted. A survey that starts from "let's ask about X" rather than "we will measure construct Y in population Z" produces items that do not add up to an answer.

Two framing choices follow. Descriptive or analytic: a descriptive survey estimates a population value (what proportion prefers A), while an analytic survey examines relationships (does satisfaction differ by segment), and the analytic goal changes the required sample and the analysis plan. Mode: web, phone, in-person, mail, or mixed. Mode is not a convenience — it determines who can be reached, how they respond, and the error structure. Web is cheap and fast but excludes those without internet; phone reaches more but suffers falling response; in-person costs most and reaches furthest. Mixed-mode designs widen coverage but introduce mode effects, where the same question is answered differently by mode.

Primary sources: <https://www.pewresearch.org/our-methods/> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survey_methodology>.

## Decision rules

- Write the construct and the population as one sentence before drafting any item.
- Declare descriptive or analytic; the goal drives sample size and analysis.
- Choose the mode by who must be reached, not by what is cheapest.
- Treat mode as part of the measurement: a mixed-mode survey must test for mode effects.
- State the four error sources (coverage, sampling, measurement, non-response) the design must address.
- A survey without a stated construct is not yet designed.

## Procedure

1. Write the research question as construct + population.
2. Mark the survey descriptive or analytic.
3. Enumerate who must be reached and how they can be contacted.
4. Choose the mode, or the mixed-mode combination, with the coverage rationale.
5. Note the mode's known effects on response and measurement.
6. Record the framing decision and the four error sources to be managed.

## Anti-patterns

- Starting from a topic instead of a measurable construct.
- Choosing web-only because it is cheap, then claiming a population result.
- Ignoring mode effects in a mixed-mode design.
- Confusing a descriptive estimate with an analytic claim.
- No stated error budget before fieldwork.
