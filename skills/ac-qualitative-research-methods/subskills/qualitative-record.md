# Qualitative record

## Standard

The qualitative record makes the study inspectable end to end. It states the research question, the design family and paradigm, the setting and participants, the sampling strategy and its adequacy, the collection methods and instruments, the analytic logic and the coding trail, the saturation claim and its evidence, the trustworthiness measures, and the researcher's reflexive account. The findings are presented with the data that ground them — the extracts, field-note excerpts, and negative cases — so a reader can see how the interpretation was reached.

The record is completed against a reporting checklist appropriate to the design: COREQ for interviews and focus groups, SRQR for qualitative research generally. The checklist is a floor for transparency, not a substitute for a coherent account. The limitations section states what the design cannot support: the contexts the findings do not transfer to, the categories left thin, the influence the researcher could not rule out. Those limits belong in the main record, not in a footnote.

Primary sources: <https://doi.org/10.1093/intqhc/mzm042> and <https://doi.org/10.1097/ACM.0000000000000388>.

## Decision rules

- Record the question, design, paradigm, setting, and participants.
- Show the sampling strategy and its adequacy, and the collection trail.
- Present the analytic logic and the coding trail with grounding extracts.
- State the saturation claim and its evidence.
- Report trustworthiness measures and the reflexive account.
- Complete the appropriate checklist; put limitations in the main record.

## Procedure

1. Assemble the question, design, paradigm, and setting.
2. Document sampling, collection, and the analytic trail.
3. Present findings with their grounding extracts and negative cases.
4. State saturation and the trustworthiness measures.
5. Complete COREQ or SRQR against the design.
6. Write the limitations in the main record.

## Anti-patterns

- Findings presented without the extracts that ground them.
- A checklist completed mechanically with no coherent account.
- Saturation and trustworthiness asserted without evidence.
- Reflexivity and limitations relegated to a footnote.
- A record that cannot show how the interpretation was reached.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Question, design, paradigm, setting, participants recorded.
- [ ] Sampling and collection trail documented.
- [ ] Analytic trail shown with grounding extracts.
- [ ] Saturation and trustworthiness evidenced.
- [ ] Checklist completed against the design.
- [ ] Limitations in the main record.

The record also preserves the decisions that did not make it into the final account: the codes that were merged or discarded, the candidate themes that failed review, and the interpretations that were considered and set aside. An audit trail that shows only the successful path understates the reasoning the account required, and a reader assessing confirmability needs to see the alternatives the analysis weighed before it settled.
