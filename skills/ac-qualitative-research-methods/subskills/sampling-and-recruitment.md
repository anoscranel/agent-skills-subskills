# Sampling and recruitment

## Standard

Qualitative sampling is purposive: participants are chosen because they can illuminate the phenomenon, not because they represent a population. The goal is information richness, and the logic is deliberate selection — typical cases, extreme or deviant cases, maximum variation across the dimensions that matter, or cases that confirm and disconfirm the emerging account. A random or convenience sample that happens to be easy to reach answers a different, weaker question than the one a purposive design asks.

Recruitment continues until the account is adequate, judged as information power rather than a target number. The more focused the question, the more specific the participants' experience, and the stronger the interview quality, the fewer participants are needed. A number fixed in advance with no reference to what the data yield is a quota, not a sampling rationale. Maximum-variation and deviant-case sampling are what let the eventual account claim breadth within its context.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualitative_research> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_study>.

## Decision rules

- Sample purposively for information richness, never for statistical representation.
- Choose the strategy: typical, extreme/deviant, maximum variation, or confirming/disconfirming.
- Judge adequacy as information power, not as a fixed participant count.
- Recruit across the dimensions that matter to the question.
- Include deviant and disconfirming cases deliberately.
- Record the sampling strategy and why each participant was included.

## Procedure

1. Identify the dimensions of experience that matter to the question.
2. Choose a purposive strategy that spans them.
3. Recruit participants who can speak to the phenomenon directly.
4. Add deviant and disconfirming cases as the account forms.
5. Assess information power as collection proceeds.
6. Record each inclusion decision and the emerging adequacy judgment.

## Anti-patterns

- A convenience sample reported as if it were purposive.
- A fixed participant count with no reference to the data.
- Sampling only easy-to-reach, similar participants.
- Excluding deviant cases because they complicate the account.
- Claiming representation from a non-probability sample.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Sampling is purposive, not probabilistic.
- [ ] Strategy spans the relevant dimensions.
- [ ] Deviant and disconfirming cases included.
- [ ] Adequacy judged as information power.
- [ ] No fixed count presented as a rationale.
- [ ] Inclusion decisions recorded.

Recruitment itself shapes the data and is recorded with the same care. How participants were approached, who declined, and any incentives offered all bear on who ends up in the study and on what they are willing to say. A gatekeeper who selects participants on the researcher's behalf can quietly narrow the sample to the cooperative or the favourable, and that narrowing belongs in the sampling account rather than being discovered after the analysis.
