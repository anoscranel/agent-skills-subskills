# Saturation and iteration

## Standard

Qualitative collection is iterative: data are collected and analysed in alternating rounds, and what the analysis reveals shapes the next round of collection. This is the collect-analyse-collect cycle, and it is what distinguishes a designed qualitative study from a batch of interviews analysed afterward. Early analysis shows which cases, settings, or questions the emerging account still needs, and the next round of sampling pursues them.

Saturation is the point at which new data no longer add new properties, dimensions, or relationships to the emerging account — not a count of interviews, and not the moment the researcher feels the data sound repetitive. It is judged against the developing categories, and it differs by category: a common experience saturates quickly; a rare or contested one needs more. Declaring saturation requires showing what was saturated, on what evidence, and acknowledging what the design could not reach. A fixed number announced in advance, or "twelve interviews and we stopped", is not a saturation argument.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grounded_theory> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualitative_research>.

## Decision rules

- Alternate collection and analysis; let early analysis guide the next round.
- Sample toward the gaps the emerging account reveals.
- Judge saturation by the account's properties, not by a participant count.
- Assess saturation per category, not only for the whole dataset.
- Show what was saturated and on what evidence.
- Name what the design could not reach or saturate.

## Procedure

1. Collect an initial round; analyse it before the next.
2. Identify the cases, settings, or questions the account still needs.
3. Sample the next round toward those gaps.
4. Track which categories are developing and which are thin.
5. Continue until new data add no new properties to the account.
6. Record the saturation claim per category with its evidence and limits.

## Anti-patterns

- Analysing all data only after all collection is complete.
- Declaring saturation from a fixed interview count.
- Stopping because the data sound repetitive, without testing against categories.
- Claiming saturation for the whole dataset while some categories are thin.
- No account of what the design could not reach.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Collection and analysis alternated.
- [ ] Later sampling guided by emerging gaps.
- [ ] Saturation judged by account properties.
- [ ] Saturation assessed per category.
- [ ] Saturation claim shown with evidence.
- [ ] Unreached areas named.
