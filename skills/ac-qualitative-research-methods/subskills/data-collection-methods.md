# Data collection methods

## Standard

The collection method follows the question and the setting. Semi-structured interviews explore experience and meaning through a flexible guide that lets the participant's account lead; observation captures what people do rather than what they say they do, in the setting itself; focus groups surface the range of views and the social dynamics between participants; documents and artifacts ground the account in what was produced independently of the research. Most strong designs combine methods so the weaknesses of one are checked by another.

The instrument is a guide, not a script. An interview guide lists the topics and probes, ordered from open to focused, and the interviewer follows the participant's meaning rather than reading questions verbatim. Recording and transcription are consented and accurate; field notes capture context, setting, and the researcher's own reactions, which are themselves data. Consent is informed, voluntary, and ongoing, and sensitive material is handled with the confidentiality the participants were promised.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focus_group> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnography>.

## Decision rules

- Match the method to the question and setting; combine methods to check weaknesses.
- Use a flexible guide, not a verbatim script, for interviews.
- Record and transcribe with consent; keep accurate field notes.
- Treat context, setting, and researcher reactions as data.
- Obtain informed, voluntary, ongoing consent; honour confidentiality.
- Pilot the guide and revise it before full collection.

## Procedure

1. Choose the collection method or mix against the question.
2. Draft the interview or observation guide, open to focused.
3. Pilot it; revise wording, order, and probes.
4. Collect with consent, recording, and field notes.
5. Transcribe accurately; note context and non-verbal detail.
6. Review early data to refine the guide for later collection.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading an interview guide as a fixed questionnaire.
- Observation reported without field notes or context.
- A focus group dominated by one voice, unmoderated.
- Recording or quoting without informed consent.
- Treating only the transcript text as data, discarding setting.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Method matched to question and setting.
- [ ] Guide is flexible, not a script.
- [ ] Piloted and revised.
- [ ] Consent, recording, field notes in place.
- [ ] Context and reactions captured.
- [ ] Early data reviewed to refine collection.

A further discipline separates what was said from what the analyst made of it. Verbatim transcription preserves the participant's words, pauses, and emphasis where they matter to the analysis, and the distinction between a quotation, a paraphrase, and the researcher's interpretation is kept visible throughout the notes. Where a translation is required, the translation choices are recorded, because meaning that the analysis depends on can be lost or introduced at that step.
