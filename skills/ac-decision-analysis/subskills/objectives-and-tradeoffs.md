# Objectives and trade-offs

## Standard

A decision is a choice among paths that score differently on things you care about. Before valuing paths, name what you care about. Objectives come in two kinds that must not be confused: fundamental objectives are ends in themselves (profit, safety, time with family), and means objectives matter only because they serve a fundamental one (market share, headcount, a launch date). Most arguments about what to do are really unexamined disagreements about which objective is fundamental, and they dissolve once that is named.

With several objectives in play, the decision needs their relative weight, and that is a judgment only the decider can supply. The craft is to elicit it honestly: rank the objectives, then price the trade-offs in concrete units ("how much profit is one month of delay worth?"). A single objective pursued to the exclusion of the rest is usually a means objective mistaken for the whole picture; naming the full set, and the weight of each, is what makes a choice defensible rather than merely felt.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_analysis> and <https://doi.org/10.2307/1909156>.

## Decision rules

- List fundamental objectives separately from means objectives.
- Ask "why does this matter?" until the answer is an end in itself.
- Elicit the decider's weights; never invent them.
- Price trade-offs in concrete units, not adjectives.
- Name the objective being sacrificed when one is maximised.
- Reject a single-metric framing when several objectives genuinely bind.

## Procedure

1. Brainstorm everything the decider cares about in this decision.
2. Separate fundamental from means objectives with "why does this matter?".
3. Rank the fundamental objectives.
4. Elicit weights or trade-off prices from the decider in concrete units.
5. Check for a hidden single-metric assumption.
6. Record the objectives, their weights, and who supplied them.

## Anti-patterns

- Optimising a means objective as if it were the goal.
- Inventing weights the decider never gave.
- Hiding a trade-off behind a vague "balanced" framing.
- Treating the loudest stakeholder's objective as the only one.
- Collapsing every objective into money when some do not convert.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Fundamental and means objectives separated.
- [ ] Fundamental objectives ranked.
- [ ] Weights elicited from the decider, in concrete units.
- [ ] Trade-offs priced, not adjectived.
- [ ] Sacrificed objective named.
- [ ] Objectives, weights, and their source recorded.
