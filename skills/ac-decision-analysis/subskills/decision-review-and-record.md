# Decision review and record

## Standard

A decision is only improvable if its reasoning survives to be reviewed. The decision journal is written at the moment of choice, before the outcome is known: the frame, the options considered, the objectives and weights, the probabilities assigned, the expected values, and the reasons for the choice. Written before the result, it is the only honest record of what was actually believed; reconstructed after, it is a story edited by hindsight.

The review then separates two things that are routinely confused: the quality of the decision and the quality of the outcome. A good process can produce a bad outcome, and a bad process a good one, because luck sits between them. Judging decisions by results alone teaches the wrong lessons and rewards the wrong behaviour. The fair review asks whether the process was sound given what was known at the time, and whether the probabilities were calibrated. Over many decisions, the record reveals systematic bias, the recurring over-optimism or the base rate that was never checked, and feeds better estimates next time.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superforecasting> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_analysis>.

## Decision rules

- Write the journal at decision time, never reconstructed after.
- Record frame, options, objectives, probabilities, values, and reasons.
- Review process quality separately from outcome quality.
- Judge the decision by what was knowable then, not by the result.
- Score calibration across many decisions, not one.
- Feed recurring biases back into the next estimate.

## Procedure

1. At decision time, record the full basis for the choice.
2. Date the entry and note what was expected to happen.
3. At review, compare the process against what was then knowable.
4. Score the outcome separately from the process.
5. Across entries, check calibration and recurring bias.
6. Update estimating practice and record the lesson.

## Anti-patterns

- Reconstructing the reasoning after the outcome is known.
- Judging a sound decision bad because it lost.
- Judging a reckless decision good because it won.
- Reviewing one decision instead of the calibration across many.
- Recording the choice but not the reasons and probabilities.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Journal written at decision time.
- [ ] Full basis recorded including probabilities.
- [ ] Process and outcome reviewed separately.
- [ ] Decision judged by then-knowable information.
- [ ] Calibration scored across entries.
- [ ] Lessons fed back into estimating.
