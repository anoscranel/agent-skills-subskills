# Value of information and sensitivity

## Standard

More information is not always worth having. The value of information is the increase in expected value from deciding after learning something rather than deciding now; it is worth buying only when it can change the choice and its benefit exceeds its cost in money, time, and delay. Information that would not change the decision is worthless for the decision, however interesting. The expected value of perfect information, the gain from knowing the uncertain quantity exactly, is the ceiling that bounds what any real research could be worth; if even perfect knowledge would not change the choice, stop researching and decide.

Sensitivity analysis answers the companion question: what does the answer hinge on? Vary each uncertain input across its stated range and watch whether the preferred option flips. The inputs that flip the decision are the load-bearing ones; they deserve the research budget and the careful elicitation. The inputs that change nothing can be left rough. A robust option, one that stays acceptable across the plausible range, is often worth more than the optimal option at a single guessed point, because it does not depend on being right about the number nobody actually knows.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_of_information> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitivity_analysis>.

## Decision rules

- Buy information only if it can change the choice and beats its cost.
- Compute the value of perfect information as the research ceiling.
- Stop researching when even perfect knowledge would not flip the choice.
- Vary each input across its range and watch for a flipped preference.
- Spend elicitation effort only on load-bearing inputs.
- Prefer a robust option over a point-optimal fragile one.

## Procedure

1. Identify the uncertain inputs the decision depends on.
2. Estimate the value of perfect information for the key unknown.
3. Compare against the cost and delay of obtaining it.
4. Run each input across its range; record which flip the decision.
5. Direct any further research only at the load-bearing inputs.
6. State whether the chosen option is robust or point-optimal.

## Anti-patterns

- Researching to reduce discomfort rather than to change a choice.
- Polishing an estimate that cannot flip the decision.
- Treating the point-optimal option as best when inputs are guessed.
- Ignoring the cost of delay while gathering information.
- Skipping sensitivity analysis because the expected value looked clear.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Load-bearing inputs identified by sensitivity.
