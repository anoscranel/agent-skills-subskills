# Decision analysis

## Overview

A consequential decision is a bet made before the facts are in. The craft of decision analysis is to structure that bet so the reasoning is visible, the uncertainty is honest, and the choice can be defended before the outcome is known. It is not the craft of being right; a good decision can lose and a bad one can win, and confusing the two is the central error this skill exists to prevent.

The neighbours each own a different moment. ac-ideation generates the options; ac-interrogation attacks a plan that already exists; ac-behavioral-investing manages the investor's biases; ac-scenario-and-stress-testing stresses a portfolio. This skill owns the moment of choosing: framing the decision, naming what you actually want, laying out real alternatives, putting honest probabilities on what you do not know, computing what each path is worth, deciding whether more information is worth its cost, and writing down why you chose so the review is fair later.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_analysis> and <https://doi.org/10.2307/1909156>.

## When to use

- A consequential, hard-to-reverse choice with real uncertainty and more than one defensible path.
- When the trade-offs between competing objectives must be made explicit.
- When you must decide whether to gather more information or commit now.
- When a past decision needs a fair process-versus-outcome review.

## When NOT to use

- To generate the option set from scratch (use ac-ideation).
- To stress-test a plan you have already chosen (use ac-interrogation).
- For investor-specific bias and journaling discipline (use ac-behavioral-investing).
- For portfolio scenario and stress arithmetic (use ac-scenario-and-stress-testing).

## Status and honest limits

Decision analysis structures judgment; it does not replace it. The probabilities are elicited from people and carry their biases, so a precise expected value built on guessed probabilities is false precision, not rigour. A model of a decision is only as good as the option set and the objectives it encodes, and the most common failure is solving the wrong decision well. Nothing here predicts outcomes or guarantees a good result; the method buys a defensible process and a fair review, not certainty. Where the choice is reversible and cheap, the full apparatus is overkill, and the skill says so.
