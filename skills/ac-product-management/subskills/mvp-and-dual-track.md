# MVP and dual-track

## Standard

The minimum viable product is the smallest thing that produces validated learning about the riskiest assumption, not the smallest sellable product and not a stripped-down version of the full vision. Its purpose is to test whether the problem and the approach are real before the team commits to building the whole. Scope is chosen by what must be learned, and everything that does not serve that learning is cut.

Dual-track agile runs discovery and delivery as two continuous streams rather than as a phase that ends. The discovery track keeps testing the next opportunities while the delivery track builds what has been validated, so learning never stops when coding starts. The discipline is that only validated work crosses into delivery, and the discovery track feeds it a steady, evidence-backed pipeline.

Primary sources: <https://www.svpg.com/dual-track-agile/> and <https://theleanstartup.com/>.

## Decision rules

- Define the MVP by the riskiest assumption to test, not by a fraction of the feature set.
- Cut everything that does not serve the learning the MVP exists to produce.
- Run discovery and delivery as continuous parallel tracks, not sequential phases.
- Only validated work crosses from discovery into delivery.
- Use cheap tests (concierge, fake door, prototype) before building the real thing.
- Treat the MVP as a learning instrument whose result may be to stop.

## Procedure

1. Name the riskiest assumption the MVP must test.
2. Scope the smallest build or test that produces evidence about it.
3. Choose the cheapest adequate test before committing to a full build.
4. Define in advance what result validates, reframes, or kills the idea.
5. Run discovery continuously alongside delivery, feeding validated work across.
6. Record what the MVP taught and the decision it drove.

## Anti-patterns

- An MVP that is just the full product with features removed at random.
- Building the real thing before testing the assumption that could kill it.
- Discovery that stops the moment delivery begins.
- Unvalidated work flowing straight into the delivery backlog.
- An MVP with no pre-stated criterion for what counts as learning.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Riskiest assumption named.
- [ ] MVP scoped to test that assumption only.
- [ ] Cheapest adequate test chosen first.
- [ ] Validation and kill criteria stated in advance.
- [ ] Discovery running in parallel with delivery.
- [ ] MVP learning and resulting decision recorded.
