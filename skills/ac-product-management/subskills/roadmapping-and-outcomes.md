# Roadmapping and outcomes

## Standard

A roadmap is a statement of intent about outcomes, not a committed list of features with dates. The outcome-based roadmap organises work around the problems to solve and the results to achieve — move this metric, fix this user struggle — rather than around a catalogue of features that commits the team to solutions before the evidence is in. The purpose is to communicate direction and priority, and to make the intended change legible to everyone who depends on it.

The now-next-later structure replaces the calendar: what we are doing now, what comes next, what is on the horizon, with confidence decreasing across the bands. Themes and outcomes sit above features, so the roadmap survives contact with reality. When a feature is listed, it is a candidate solution to a named outcome, not a promise, and the roadmap is expected to change as discovery evidence arrives.

Primary sources: <https://www.producttalk.org/> and <https://www.svpg.com/>.

## Decision rules

- Frame the roadmap around outcomes and problems, not a dated feature list.
- Use now-next-later bands with confidence decreasing across them.
- Keep themes and outcomes above features; features are candidate solutions.
- Make each outcome measurable, so the roadmap's success can be checked.
- Revisit and change the roadmap as evidence arrives; it is intent, not a contract.
- Communicate what is deliberately out of scope, not only what is in.

## Procedure

1. State the outcomes the product must achieve, in measurable terms.
2. Group candidate work into themes tied to those outcomes.
3. Place work into now, next, and later bands by priority and confidence.
4. Mark features as candidate solutions, not commitments.
5. Record what is explicitly out of scope and why.
6. Review on a cadence and after material evidence, and revise openly.

## Anti-patterns

- A dated feature list presented as a commitment to customers.
- Treating the roadmap as fixed, so new evidence cannot change it.
- Outcomes with no measure, so success cannot be checked.
- Every item in the now band, which means nothing is prioritised.
- A roadmap that hides what was cut, inviting constant re-litigation.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Outcomes stated in measurable terms.
- [ ] Work grouped into themes tied to outcomes.
- [ ] Now-next-later bands set by priority and confidence.
- [ ] Features marked as candidate solutions.
- [ ] Out-of-scope items and reasons recorded.
- [ ] Review cadence and revision process stated.
