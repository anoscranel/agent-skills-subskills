# Customer discovery and evidence

## Standard

Customer discovery tests the framing against reality before resources are committed. The core discipline is that opinion is cheap and behaviour is evidence: what customers actually do, pay for, and struggle with predicts demand far better than what they say they would do. The interview is a tool for learning, not for pitching, and the goal is to find out whether the problem is real, frequent, and painful enough to pay to solve.

The method that works is the Mom Test: ask about the customer's past behaviour and current situation, never about your idea or their hypothetical future. "Tell me about the last time you hit this problem" yields evidence; "would you use a tool that does X" yields polite lies. Strong signals are commitment and sacrifice — time, money, reputation already spent on the problem — not compliments.

Primary sources: <https://theleanstartup.com/> and <https://www.producttalk.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Ask about past behaviour and current situation, never hypothetical future use.
- Do not pitch the idea in a discovery interview; the goal is learning, not validation.
- Treat commitment and sacrifice (time, money, workarounds) as strong evidence, compliments as weak.
- Seek disconfirming evidence deliberately, not only support for the framing.
- Distinguish the problem being real from the proposed solution being wanted.
- Continue until the evidence is consistent enough to act on, and record what would change the decision.

## Procedure

1. Write the assumptions to test, most decision-critical first.
2. Recruit interviewees who match the target user and circumstance.
3. Ask about the last specific instance of the problem and how it was handled.
4. Probe for existing workarounds, spending, and frequency.
5. Log evidence for and against each assumption, with direct observations.
6. Revisit the framing and decide: proceed, reframe, or stop, and record why.

## Anti-patterns

- Asking "would you use this" and counting the yes as demand.
- Pitching the solution and measuring the interviewee's politeness.
- Interviewing only friendly contacts who want the project to succeed.
- Counting compliments as evidence of willingness to pay.
- Stopping at the first supportive answer and ignoring disconfirming cases.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Assumptions to test listed, most critical first.
- [ ] Interviewees match the target user and circumstance.
- [ ] Questions asked about past behaviour, not hypotheticals.
- [ ] Commitment and sacrifice signals sought and recorded.
- [ ] Disconfirming evidence logged alongside support.
- [ ] Decision to proceed, reframe, or stop recorded with reasons.
