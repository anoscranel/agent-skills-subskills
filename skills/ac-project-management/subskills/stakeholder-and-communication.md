# Stakeholder and communication

## Standard

Stakeholders are everyone who affects, or is affected by, the project. They are analysed by interest and influence so that engagement effort goes where it is needed: high-influence, high-interest stakeholders are managed closely, and the rest are kept informed at a level that matches their stake. A stakeholder no one identified is the one who derails the project late.

The communication plan states who needs what information, in what form, at what frequency, and from whom. Status reporting is honest and regular, reporting progress against the baseline rather than a narrative of effort. Bad news travels early; a stakeholder surprised by a slip at the end is a failure of the communication plan, not of the work. The plan is reviewed as stakeholders and their influence change over the project's life.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stakeholder_management> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_management>.

## Decision rules

- Identify all stakeholders, including those with indirect or negative interest.
- Map each by interest and influence; manage the high-high quadrant closely.
- State who needs what, in what form, how often, and from whom, in a written plan.
- Report progress against the baseline, not as a narrative of effort.
- Surface bad news early; no surprises at the end.
- Revisit the analysis as stakeholders and their influence change.

## Procedure

1. List stakeholders and their interests in the project.
2. Map each on the interest-influence grid.
3. Choose an engagement strategy per quadrant.
4. Write the communication plan: content, form, frequency, owner.
5. Set the status-reporting cadence and format.
6. Review the map and plan at each phase change.

## Anti-patterns

- Stakeholders identified only among the obvious supporters.
- A communication plan that says only "keep everyone informed".
- Status reports that describe activity instead of baseline progress.
- Bad news withheld until it can no longer be hidden.
- A stakeholder map written once and never revisited.

## Done checklist

- [ ] All stakeholders identified.
- [ ] Interest-influence mapping done.
- [ ] Engagement strategy per quadrant.
- [ ] Communication plan written with owners.
- [ ] Reporting cadence and format set.
- [ ] Map and plan reviewed on change.

The interest-influence grid is a starting model, not a diagnosis. Influence is often informal, held by someone without a senior title who shapes opinion, and interest shifts as the project touches people's work. The map is most useful when it is challenged in conversation rather than assumed, and when it is updated as the project moves through phases and different stakeholders gain or lose a reason to care.
