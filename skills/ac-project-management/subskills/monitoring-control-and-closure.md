# Monitoring, control and closure

## Standard

Monitoring compares actual progress against the baseline so that variance is caught early. Earned value management integrates scope, schedule and cost into three numbers: planned value is the work that should be done, earned value is the work actually done, and actual cost is what it cost. From these, schedule and cost variance and the performance indices tell you whether the project is ahead or behind, and roughly by how much, in terms a status report can act on.

Change control is the gate through which every proposed change to scope, schedule or budget must pass, with its impact assessed before approval. Closure is a deliberate act, not a fade: deliverables are accepted against the success criteria set in the charter, contracts are closed, and lessons are recorded while they are fresh. A project that ends without a closure review teaches its mistakes to the next one.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earned_value_management> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_management>.

## Decision rules

- Track planned value, earned value and actual cost against the baseline.
- Report schedule and cost variance with the performance indices, not just effort spent.
- Route every scope, schedule or budget change through change control with impact assessed.
- Accept deliverables against the charter's success criteria at closure.
- Record lessons learned while the project is fresh.
- Close contracts and release resources deliberately, not by default.

## Procedure

1. Measure planned value, earned value and actual cost on cadence.
2. Compute schedule and cost variance and the performance indices.
3. Report variance with its causes and the corrective action.
4. Assess and decide each change through change control.
5. Verify deliverables against the success criteria at closure.
6. Record lessons learned and close contracts and resources.

## Anti-patterns

- Reporting percent-complete by gut feel instead of earned value.
- Variance reported without its cause or a corrective action.
- Changes absorbed informally with no impact assessment.
- Declaring closure when the team simply stopped working.
- Lessons learned skipped because the next project has started.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Planned value, earned value and actual cost tracked.
- [ ] Variance and indices computed and reported.
- [ ] Corrective actions tied to variance.
- [ ] Changes passed through change control.
- [ ] Deliverables accepted against success criteria.
- [ ] Lessons recorded and resources released.

The indices also forecast, not just report. A cost performance index below one means the project is spending more than it earns for the work done, and projecting that rate forward gives an estimate at completion that can be compared against the budget while there is still time to act.
