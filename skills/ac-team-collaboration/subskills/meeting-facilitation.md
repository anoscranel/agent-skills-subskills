# Meeting facilitation

## Standard

A meeting is the most expensive way a team communicates: an hour with eight people is a person-day. It is justified only when the work needs real-time interaction — a decision that needs debate, a conflict to resolve, a complex problem to untangle. Status updates, announcements, and anything one person could write are almost never a good reason to meet. The default question before scheduling is "what interaction does this need that a document cannot provide?" — and if the answer is none, the meeting becomes a doc.

A meeting that does happen is run deliberately: a stated purpose and desired outcome, an agenda shared in advance, only the people the interaction needs, a facilitator who keeps it on track and draws out the quiet voices, and a close that captures decisions and named actions with owners and dates. A meeting that ends without recorded decisions and actions produced discussion, not progress.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meeting> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facilitator>.

## Decision rules

- Justify each meeting by the interaction it needs; convert status and announcements to writing.
- State the purpose and desired outcome on the invite, with the agenda in advance.
- Invite only the people the interaction requires; make others optional with notes.
- Assign a facilitator to hold the thread and include the quiet participants.
- Time-box each item; park tangents into a visible list rather than losing them.
- Close by recording decisions and actions with owners and dates, and circulate them.

## Procedure

1. Ask what interaction the topic needs; if none, write a document instead.
2. State purpose, desired outcome, and agenda on the invite; share pre-reads early.
3. Invite the minimum necessary; assign a facilitator and a note-taker.
4. Run to the agenda, time-box items, and park tangents visibly.
5. Close with decisions and actions, each with an owner and a date.
6. Circulate notes the same day; review recurring meetings for continued value.

## Anti-patterns

- A recurring status meeting that survives because it has always existed.
- No agenda, or an agenda of vague topics with no desired outcome.
- Half the room silent while two people talk.
- Ending with "good discussion" and no recorded decision or action.
- Inviting everyone "just in case" so nobody feels able to decline.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Interaction justified; status moved to writing.
- [ ] Purpose, outcome, agenda, pre-reads shared in advance.
- [ ] Minimum necessary attendees; facilitator and note-taker assigned.
- [ ] Time-boxed; tangents parked.
- [ ] Decisions and actions recorded with owners and dates.
- [ ] Notes circulated; recurring meetings reviewed for value.
