# Conflict and repair

## Standard

Conflict in a team is information: it signals a real disagreement about the work, a clash of needs, or a breach of expectations. A team with no visible conflict is usually suppressing it, and suppressed conflict does not disappear — it goes underground into passive resistance, gossip, or quiet disengagement. The goal is not to eliminate conflict but to make it productive: surface it early, keep it about the work, and resolve it before it becomes personal.

The distinction that matters is task conflict from relationship conflict. Disagreement about what to do is healthy and often improves the decision; disagreement that becomes about who the other person is corrodes trust and must be repaired. Repair is a deliberate act: acknowledge the rupture, take responsibility for your part, listen to the other person's account without re-litigating, and agree on how to work together going forward. A relationship left unrepaired after a rupture becomes a permanent tax on every future interaction.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_resolution> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_safety>.

## Decision rules

- Surface disagreement early; treat it as information about the work, not as failure.
- Keep conflict about the task; name and stop any slide into the personal.
- Listen to the other account fully before defending your own.
- After a rupture, acknowledge it and take responsibility for your part first.
- Agree explicitly how you will work together going forward.
- Escalate to a neutral party when the two of you cannot resolve it alone.

## Procedure

1. Notice and name the disagreement early, before it hardens.
2. Frame it as a shared problem to solve, not a contest to win.
3. Hear the other account fully; use active listening and check you understood.
4. Find the underlying needs and interests, not just the stated positions.
5. If trust broke, acknowledge the rupture and own your part before anything else.
6. Agree the way forward and the escalation path if it recurs.

## Anti-patterns

- Avoiding the conflict until it becomes a crisis.
- Letting a task disagreement turn into a personal attack.
- Winning the argument and losing the working relationship.
- A forced apology with no real acknowledgment of the rupture.
- Assuming time alone will repair a breach of trust.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Disagreement surfaced early and framed as shared problem.
- [ ] Kept to task; personal slides stopped.
- [ ] Both accounts heard and understood.
- [ ] Ruptures acknowledged; own part owned first.
- [ ] Way forward agreed explicitly.
- [ ] Escalation path named for the unresolvable.
