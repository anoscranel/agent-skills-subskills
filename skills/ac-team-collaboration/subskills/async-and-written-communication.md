# Async and written communication

## Standard

Async-first communication means the default way work moves forward is written and read on each person's own schedule, not synchronously in real time. For a distributed team across time zones it is a necessity, not a preference: a synchronous-only team forces someone to always work at a bad hour and makes every decision hostage to who was awake. Async also produces a written record, which is how decisions become findable by people who were not there.

The craft is choosing the channel by urgency and richness. Write the decision down where it can be found, with the context and reasoning a future reader needs. Reserve synchronous time for what genuinely needs it — a sensitive conversation, a fast-moving incident, a relationship that needs repair. A team that goes async-first without writing down decisions does not become efficient; it becomes a team where no one can find why anything was decided.

Primary sources: <https://about.gitlab.com/company/culture/all-remote/asynchronous/> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team>.

## Decision rules

- Default to written, asynchronous communication; make it findable later.
- Record each decision with its context and reasoning, not just the outcome.
- Choose the channel by urgency and richness: urgent and sensitive go synchronous, the rest async.
- Respect time zones: no expectation of instant replies; state response-time norms.
- Write for the reader who was not present and lacks your context.
- Keep synchronous time for what needs real-time interaction; protect it.

## Procedure

1. Set the default: work moves forward in writing, on each person's schedule.
2. Choose a canonical place for decisions; write each with context and reasoning.
3. Define response-time norms per channel so "async" is not read as "ignored".
4. Move status and updates to written form; reserve meetings for interaction.
5. Use synchronous time deliberately for sensitive or urgent matters.
6. Review periodically whether decisions are findable by someone new.

## Anti-patterns

- "Async" used to mean "I will respond whenever, with no norms".
- Decisions made in a chat scroll and never written anywhere findable.
- Expecting instant replies across time zones.
- A wall of text with no context, forcing the reader to reconstruct the situation.
- Eliminating all synchronous time, including the conversations that need it.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Written async is the default.
- [ ] Decisions recorded with context and reasoning.
- [ ] Channel chosen by urgency and richness.
- [ ] Response-time norms stated per channel.
- [ ] Writing assumes an absent reader.
- [ ] Synchronous time protected for what needs it.
