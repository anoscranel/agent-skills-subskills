# Working agreements and norms

## Standard

A working agreement is a short, written set of norms the team itself has chosen about how it works: how decisions get made, how the team communicates, what a meeting is for, how conflict is raised, what "done" means. Most team friction comes from norms that exist but were never stated — one person assumes decisions are consensus, another assumes the lead decides; one expects instant replies, another batches them. Making the norms explicit turns silent mismatches into a shared, revisable agreement.

The agreement is built by the team, not handed down: a norm imposed without discussion is a rule that gets quietly ignored. It stays short — a page of real commitments beats ten of aspirations — and it is revisited when the team changes, because a norm that fit five people breaks at fifteen. Each norm names the behaviour and the reason, so a newcomer can follow the intent, not just the letter.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facilitator>.

## Decision rules

- Write the agreement with the team, never impose it; unowned norms are ignored.
- Cover the recurring friction points: decision-making, communication, meetings, conflict, "done".
- State each norm as a concrete behaviour plus its reason, not a value word.
- Keep it to one page of real commitments; cut anything no one will enforce.
- Revisit the agreement whenever the team's size, composition, or mission changes.
- A norm nobody follows is renegotiated or dropped, not left as decoration.

## Procedure

1. Gather the team; list the recurring friction points and unwritten assumptions.
2. For each, agree the norm as a concrete behaviour and record the reason.
3. Make the decision-making rule explicit: who decides, and how disagreement is handled.
4. Write the agreement in one page; circulate for comment and adopt it.
5. Schedule a revisit on team change or a fixed cadence.
6. When a norm is repeatedly broken, renegotiate or drop it openly.

## Anti-patterns

- A values poster ("respect, ownership, excellence") with no behaviour attached.
- Norms written by the lead and announced, never adopted.
- A twenty-page agreement nobody reads.
- Leaving a broken norm in place to avoid the conversation.
- Assuming the norms are obvious and need no writing.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Agreement written with the team, not imposed.
- [ ] Decision-making, communication, meetings, conflict, "done" covered.
- [ ] Each norm is a behaviour plus a reason.
- [ ] One page; no unenforced norms.
- [ ] Revisit scheduled on team change.
- [ ] Broken norms renegotiated openly.
