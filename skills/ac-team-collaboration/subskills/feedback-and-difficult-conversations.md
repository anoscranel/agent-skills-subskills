# Feedback and difficult conversations

## Standard

Feedback is information about the gap between intended and actual effect, given so the person can adjust. Useful feedback is specific and about observable behaviour, not character: "you interrupted three times in the review" can be acted on, "you are dismissive" cannot. The widely used frame is Situation-Behaviour-Impact — name when and where, what the person did, and the effect it had — which keeps the conversation on what happened rather than on who the person is. Timeliness matters: feedback given weeks later loses both accuracy and usefulness.

Difficult conversations are best had early and directly. The common failure is avoidance: a small irritation is swallowed until it becomes a blow-up or a quiet resentment. The craft is to raise the issue while it is small, to separate the person from the problem, and to describe your own observation and reaction rather than attributing motive. Nonviolent Communication's structure — observation, feeling, need, request — gives a way to be honest without being attacking.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedback> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_listening>.

## Decision rules

- Give feedback on observable behaviour, never on character or assumed motive.
- Use Situation-Behaviour-Impact to keep it concrete and actionable.
- Deliver feedback promptly; stale feedback is less accurate and less useful.
- Raise difficult issues early, while they are small, directly with the person.
- Separate the person from the problem; describe your observation and reaction.
- Invite feedback yourself and receive it without defensiveness.

## Procedure

1. Collect the specific observations: when, where, what behaviour, what impact.
2. Choose a private setting and a moment when both can talk.
3. State the observation, its impact, and your reaction without attribution.
4. Make a concrete request for what to do differently, and listen to their view.
5. Agree on the next step and follow up.
6. Ask for feedback on your own behaviour regularly; thank the giver.

## Anti-patterns

- Feedback about personality ("you are careless") instead of behaviour.
- Saving up grievances for a single explosive conversation.
- The feedback sandwich that buries the point between compliments.
- Raising a hard issue with everyone except the person involved.
- Asking for feedback, then arguing with the giver.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Feedback is behaviour-specific and actionable.
- [ ] Situation-Behaviour-Impact used.
- [ ] Delivered promptly and privately.
- [ ] Hard issues raised early and directly.
- [ ] Person separated from problem; no motive attribution.
- [ ] Own feedback invited and received without defensiveness.
