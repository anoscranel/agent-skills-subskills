# Stakeholder management and alignment

## Standard

A stakeholder is anyone who can affect or is affected by the work: the sponsor who funds it, the team that builds it, the adjacent team it touches, the user it serves. Work fails as often from misaligned stakeholders as from bad execution — a sponsor who expected something different, an adjacent team blindsided by a dependency, a decision-maker who learned of a change too late. Stakeholder management is the deliberate work of knowing who these people are, what they need, and keeping them aligned as the work changes.

The craft starts with mapping: who has interest, and who has influence, because the people you must keep closest are those with both. Alignment is then explicit, not assumed: agree the goal and the success criteria up front, manage expectations as reality diverges from the plan, and surface competing priorities for a decision rather than quietly dropping one. Proactive communication — telling people before they ask — is what keeps a stakeholder a supporter rather than a surprise.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stakeholder_management> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team>.

## Decision rules

- Map stakeholders by interest and influence; invest most where both are high.
- Agree the goal and the success criteria explicitly at the start.
- Communicate proactively; let no stakeholder learn of a change by accident.
- Manage expectations as reality diverges from the plan, early and honestly.
- Surface competing priorities for an explicit decision, not a silent trade-off.
- Revisit the map as the work and the organisation change.

## Procedure

1. List everyone who affects or is affected by the work.
2. Map them by interest and influence; choose the engagement level for each.
3. Agree goals, success criteria, and decision rights with the key stakeholders.
4. Set a communication cadence proportionate to each stakeholder's stake.
5. Report divergences from plan early, with options, not just problems.
6. Re-map when scope, leadership, or dependencies change.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating alignment as achieved because no one objected in a meeting.
- Surprising a high-influence stakeholder with a decision already made.
- Managing only the loud stakeholders and ignoring the quiet influential ones.
- Quietly dropping a priority rather than escalating the conflict.
- Communicating only when there is good news.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Stakeholders mapped by interest and influence.
- [ ] Goals and success criteria agreed explicitly.
- [ ] Decision rights clear.
- [ ] Proactive cadence set per stakeholder.
- [ ] Divergences surfaced early with options.
- [ ] Map revisited on change.
