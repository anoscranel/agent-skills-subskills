# Audience and purpose

## Standard

Before any science content is written, name the audience precisely and what they should take away. "The general public" is not an audience; "parents deciding whether to vaccinate", "a journalist on a deadline", "a parliamentary aide briefing a minister" are. The audience decides the prior knowledge assumed, the frame that makes the finding relevant, and the action or understanding the piece aims for.

The classic trap is the deficit model: the assumption that the audience lacks facts and that supplying them produces understanding and agreement. Decades of research show facts alone rarely change minds; people interpret information through prior beliefs and values. The alternative is a dialogue or framing approach: meet the audience where they are, connect the science to what they already care about, and invite engagement rather than lecture. The purpose is stated as what the audience will understand, feel equipped to decide, or be able to explain — not as "raise awareness".

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_understanding_of_science> and <https://www.nap.edu/catalog/23674/communicating-science-effectively-a-research-agenda>.

## Decision rules

- Name the audience as a specific group with a situation, not "the public".
- State the takeaway as what they will understand or be able to do.
- Reject the pure deficit model; facts alone do not change minds.
- Frame the science around what the audience already values.
- Assume the audience's prior knowledge honestly; do not over- or under-estimate it.
- One primary audience per piece; a second audience gets its own version.

## Procedure

1. Name the audience group and their situation.
2. Write what they already know and believe about the topic.
3. State the single takeaway in their terms.
4. Choose the frame that connects the science to their concerns.
5. Decide the format and channel that reaches them.
6. Check the plan against the deficit-model trap.

## Anti-patterns

- "For everyone" as the target audience.
- Assuming more facts will settle a contested topic.
- Framing around the researcher's interest, not the audience's.
- Underestimating what a lay audience can grasp with good explanation.
- Overestimating prior knowledge and skipping needed context.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Specific audience and situation named.
- [ ] Prior knowledge and beliefs considered.
- [ ] Single takeaway in the audience's terms.
- [ ] Frame connects to audience concerns.
- [ ] Deficit model rejected.
- [ ] Format and channel chosen for that audience.
