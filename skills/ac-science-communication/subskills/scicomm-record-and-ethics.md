# Scicomm record and ethics

## Standard

Science communication carries responsibilities beyond good writing: accuracy about the evidence, honesty about uncertainty, and accountability when things go wrong. The record captures the source study and its actual claims, the audience and purpose, the editorial choices made and why, and the checks the piece passed. It allows a later reader to see what the public was told and on what basis.

Corrections are a strength, not a failure. When a piece is wrong, or the underlying finding is corrected or retracted, the record and the public piece are updated promptly and visibly. Responsible communication also means not amplifying misinformation: debunking is done carefully, because repeating a false claim can entrench it, so the correction leads with the fact and treats the myth as the thing being replaced. The communicator's duty is to the public's understanding, not to the institution's image or the story's reach.

Primary sources: <https://senseaboutscience.org/> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_communication>.

## Decision rules

- Record the source study and its actual claims alongside the piece.
- Log the editorial choices and the accuracy checks performed.
- Correct errors and retracted findings promptly and visibly.
- Lead a debunk with the fact; do not centre the myth.
- The duty is to public understanding, not institutional image.
- Never amplify a false claim for the sake of engagement.

## Procedure

1. Archive the source study and its claims with the piece.
2. Record audience, purpose and editorial choices.
3. Log the accuracy and uncertainty checks.
4. Monitor for corrections or retraction of the source.
5. Update or correct the public piece visibly when needed.
6. Review what the public was actually told and learned.

## Anti-patterns

- No record linking the public claim to the source study.
- Silent edits that hide a correction.
- Repeating the myth prominently in a debunk.
- Leaving a retracted finding's coverage uncorrected.
- Prioritising reach or image over accuracy.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Source study and claims archived.
- [ ] Audience, purpose and choices recorded.
- [ ] Accuracy and uncertainty checks logged.
- [ ] Corrections and retractions monitored.
- [ ] Public piece corrected visibly when needed.
- [ ] Debunks lead with the fact, not the myth.

Conflicts of interest belong in the record and, where relevant, in the piece. A study funded by an interested party, or a communicator employed by the institution that produced the work, should be disclosed so the audience can weigh the source. This is not an accusation of bias; it is standard practice that protects credibility. The record also notes who reviewed the piece for accuracy and what they changed, so accountability for the public claim is traceable to a named check rather than to a general assurance.
