# Finding the story

## Standard

A study is not a story. The story is the answer to "why should this audience care, now?" The hook is the specific thing that makes the finding relevant: a problem it bears on, a surprising result, a question it settles or opens, a practical consequence. The hook is found in the finding's significance, not in its novelty alone — incremental work can matter, and a flashy result can be unimportant.

The discipline is the "so what" test. For each candidate angle, ask what changes for the reader if the finding holds. If the honest answer is "very little", the angle is wrong or the piece is not worth writing. The lede states the finding and its significance in plain terms; the detail of method and the qualifications follow. The story is built from the result and its real implications, not from the researcher's enthusiasm or the institution's desire for coverage.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_journalism> and <https://theconversation.com/us>.

## Decision rules

- The hook is the finding's significance to this audience, not its novelty.
- Apply the "so what" test; drop angles that change little for the reader.
- Lead with the finding and its meaning; method and caveats follow.
- Draw the story from the result and real implications, not enthusiasm.
- A single clear angle beats three competing ones.
- If there is no honest hook, reconsider whether the piece is warranted.

## Procedure

1. State the finding in one plain sentence.
2. List what changes for the reader if it holds.
3. Choose the angle with the strongest honest significance.
4. Write the lede as finding plus meaning.
5. Order the rest: context, evidence, caveats, implications.
6. Re-check the angle against the "so what" test.

## Anti-patterns

- Leading with the institution or the researcher, not the finding.
- Confusing a surprising result with an important one.
- Burying the significance under method detail.
- An angle chosen for coverage, not for the reader.
- A piece written because a paper exists, not because it matters.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Finding stated in one plain sentence.
- [ ] "So what" answered honestly.
- [ ] Strongest honest angle chosen.
- [ ] Lede carries finding and meaning.
- [ ] Structure is context, evidence, caveats, implications.
- [ ] Angle re-checked for genuine significance.

Timing shapes the hook as much as content does. A finding becomes a story when it connects to something the audience is already thinking about — a current debate, a seasonal concern, a decision people face. This is not chasing trends; it is choosing the moment when the finding is most useful. The evergreen angle is kept as a fallback for when the news hook fades. Either way, the significance must be real: a timely frame cannot rescue a finding that does not matter to the reader.
