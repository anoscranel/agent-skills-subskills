# Press release and media

## Standard

A press release is not a summary of the paper; it is a pitch for coverage that must stand on its own. The journalist reads it on a deadline, decides in seconds whether it is a story, and may lift wording directly. The release therefore leads with the finding and its significance in plain language, states the evidence honestly, and includes a quotable line, the key numbers and the caveats. The paper's structure — abstract, method, results — is the wrong shape; the release is organised as a news story.

Working with journalists is a distinct skill. Provide the finding, the context and an accessible expert who can explain it. Respect embargoes: they coordinate release timing, not control of the story. Expect the headline to be written by an editor, not by you, and expect simplification; the defence is a clear, accurate release and a willingness to correct errors. When a story gets the science wrong, seek a correction promptly and without defensiveness. The relationship is long-term; reliability and accuracy beat one big splash.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_journalism> and <https://sciline.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Structure the release as a news story, not a paper abstract.
- Lead with finding and significance in plain language.
- Include a quotable line, key numbers and caveats.
- Respect embargoes as timing coordination, not story control.
- Provide an accessible expert and context for the journalist.
- Seek corrections of scientific errors promptly and calmly.

## Procedure

1. Write the lede as finding plus significance.
2. Add context, evidence strength and caveats.
3. Include a quotable expert line and key numbers.
4. Set the embargo and distribute to relevant journalists.
5. Make an expert available for explanation.
6. Track coverage and request corrections where needed.

## Anti-patterns

- A release that reads like the paper's abstract.
- Hyped institutional claims the evidence does not support.
- Treating the embargo as a way to shape the story.
- Defensive responses to inaccurate coverage.
- Measuring success by coverage volume rather than accuracy.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Release structured as a news story.
- [ ] Lede carries finding and significance.
- [ ] Quote, numbers and caveats included.
- [ ] Embargo set and respected.
- [ ] Expert available for journalists.
- [ ] Coverage tracked; errors corrected.

The supporting materials matter as much as the release. A one-page lay summary, a short list of the key numbers, an image or diagram that is cleared for reuse, and a named contact who will actually respond on deadline all increase the chance of accurate coverage. Journalists work to the hour, so materials that answer the obvious follow-up questions in advance reduce the risk of errors being filled in by assumption. The goal is to make the accurate version the easiest version to publish.
