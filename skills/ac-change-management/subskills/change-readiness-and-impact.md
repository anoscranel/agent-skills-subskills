# Change Readiness And Impact

## Standard

Change-readiness assessment asks whether the organisation can absorb the change: its capacity, its history with past change, the strength of the current culture, and the credibility of the sponsors. An organisation exhausted by overlapping initiatives has little capacity left, and a history of abandoned changes teaches people to wait this one out. Readiness is assessed before the plan, because the plan's pace and method depend on it.

Change-impact analysis maps who is affected and how: which roles, which processes, which tools, which measures of a good day's work change. It distinguishes the groups for whom the change is a minor adjustment from those for whom it is a threat to competence, status, or livelihood. The impact map is the input to the communication, training, and resistance plans, and it is where a change that looks small at the top is revealed to be large at the front line.

The findings are written down, not held in the change manager's head, because they are the evidence the plan is built on and the baseline against which later resistance is understood.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change_management> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizational_change>.

## Decision rules

- Assess readiness: capacity, change history, culture, sponsor credibility.
- Map impact per group: roles, processes, tools, and what a good day looks like.
- Distinguish adjustment from threat to competence, status, or security.
- Feed the impact map into communication, training, and resistance plans.
- Calibrate pace and method to readiness; do not force one plan on all groups.
- Revisit readiness as the change unfolds; it is not a one-time rating.

## Procedure

1. Assess organisational readiness and capacity for this change.
2. Review the history of past change efforts and their credibility.
3. Map each affected group and the impact on their work.
4. Classify impacts as adjustment versus threat.
5. Set pace and method per group from the readiness and impact findings.
6. Record the assessment and revisit it at each phase.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming readiness because leadership is enthusiastic.
- A single impact assessment applied to every group.
- Ignoring the credibility damage of past failed changes.
- Treating a front-line threat as a minor adjustment.
- Assessing readiness once and never revisiting it.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Readiness and capacity assessed.
- [ ] Change history reviewed.
- [ ] Impact mapped per group.
- [ ] Adjustment versus threat distinguished.
- [ ] Pace and method calibrated per group.
- [ ] Readiness revisited on a cadence.
