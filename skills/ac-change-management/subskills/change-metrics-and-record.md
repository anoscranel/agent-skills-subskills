# Change Metrics And Record

## Standard

Change metrics measure adoption and its effects, not change-team activity. Messages sent, people trained, and sessions held are activity; the measures that matter are the behaviour change itself, the speed of adoption, the proficiency reached, and the outcome the change was meant to produce. Where the change was justified by a benefit, the metric checks whether the benefit appeared — a delivered system that is used but produces no benefit is a different problem to surface, not a success to report.

The change record is the evidence base for this and future changes: the case and readiness findings, the model and roadmap, the impact map, the communication and training delivered, the adoption measures over time, and the lessons learned. It records what worked and what did not, so the next change starts from evidence rather than from enthusiasm. Its limits are stated honestly: which groups have not adopted, which benefits have not appeared, and what remains unresolved.

The metrics themselves are chosen before the launch, because a measure invented after the fact tends to flatter the effort. Each metric names its data source, its baseline, and its target, and a small set is preferred over a dashboard nobody reads.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change_management> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADKAR>.

## Decision rules

- Measure adoption and outcomes, not change-team activity.
- Track the behaviour change, its speed, proficiency, and the intended benefit.
- Surface a used-but-no-benefit result as a problem, not a success.
- Record case, readiness, model, impact, delivery, and adoption over time.
- Capture lessons learned for the next change.
- State the record's limits: non-adopters, unrealised benefits, unresolved issues.

## Procedure

1. Define adoption and outcome metrics before the launch.
2. Measure behaviour change, speed, proficiency, and benefit over time.
3. Compare realised benefit against the case for change.
4. Assemble the record from each phase's findings and delivery.
5. Run a lessons-learned review with the change team and sponsors.
6. File the record with its limits named for the next change effort.

## Anti-patterns

- Reporting activity as if it were adoption.
- Claiming the benefit without measuring it.
- A record that only documents the launch.
- Lessons learned captured but never read.
- Limits and non-adopters omitted from the record.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Adoption and outcome metrics defined.
- [ ] Behaviour, speed, proficiency, benefit measured over time.
- [ ] Realised benefit compared to the case.
- [ ] Record assembled from all phases.
- [ ] Lessons learned reviewed and filed.
- [ ] Limits and unresolved issues named.
