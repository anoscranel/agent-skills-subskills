# Stakeholder Engagement And Communication

## Standard

A change needs a sponsor coalition, not a single sponsor: a group of credible leaders and influencers who visibly back it, model the new behaviour, and hold the line when resistance appears. Communication then answers the questions each group actually has — why this, why now, what changes for me, what stays the same, and where do I get help. It is repeated, two-way, and honest about what is not yet decided; a single launch announcement is not a communication plan.

Resistance is treated as information, not as a defect to overcome. It signals what the change threatens — competence, status, workload, or trust from past failures. The response is to listen, to involve people in shaping the parts they can shape, and to address the real concern rather than to repeat the vision louder. Some resistance is a legitimate judgment that the change is wrong for a group, and that judgment is escalated, not managed away.

Communication is also timed to the change, not to the news cycle: the right message at the moment each group must act, reinforced by their own manager, lands far better than a polished all-hands broadcast.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change_management> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kotter>.

## Decision rules

- Build a sponsor coalition, not a single sponsor; sponsors model the new behaviour.
- Communicate to each group's real questions, repeatedly and two-way.
- Be honest about what is undecided; do not over-reassure.
- Treat resistance as information about what the change threatens.
- Involve people in shaping what they can; address the real concern.
- Escalate legitimate objections rather than managing them away.

## Procedure

1. Identify and recruit the sponsor coalition.
2. Brief sponsors to model the behaviour and hold the line.
3. Plan communication per group: their questions, channels, and cadence.
4. Run two-way channels and log the concerns raised.
5. Analyse resistance for its underlying cause and respond to it.
6. Escalate objections that indicate the change itself is wrong.

## Anti-patterns

- Relying on one senior sponsor and a launch email.
- One-way communication that never answers 'what changes for me'.
- Over-reassuring on points that are not yet decided.
- Labelling all resistance as negativity to be overcome.
- Repeating the vision louder instead of addressing the concern.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Sponsor coalition recruited and briefed.
- [ ] Communication planned per group with cadence.
- [ ] Two-way channels running with concerns logged.
- [ ] Resistance analysed and addressed by cause.
- [ ] Legitimate objections escalated.
- [ ] Sponsors observed modelling the behaviour.
