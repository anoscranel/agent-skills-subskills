# Training And Adoption

## Standard

Training builds the knowledge and ability the change requires, but a course is not adoption. People need to know how to do the new work, then to do it with support until it is normal. Training is designed per role: what each group must know and be able to do, practised on their real tasks, not a generic overview of the system. Capability is measured by whether people can perform, not by attendance.

Adoption support continues after training: floor-walkers, champions, office hours, quick-reference guides, and a way to get help at the moment of need. Champions are respected members of the affected groups who support their peers and carry the front-line reality back to the change team. The measure of adoption support is whether people can get unstuck quickly, and whether the gap between the trained and the confident is closing.

The training plan is timed to the change, not to convenience: too early and the knowledge decays before it is used, too late and people are already improvising. Just-in-time training, delivered close to the moment each group starts the new work, outperforms a single big launch event.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADKAR> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change_management>.

## Decision rules

- Design training per role: what each group must know and do, on real tasks.
- Measure capability by performance, not attendance.
- Provide adoption support after training: champions, office hours, quick references.
- Recruit champions from within the affected groups.
- Carry front-line reality back through the champions.
- Measure support by how quickly people get unstuck.

## Procedure

1. Define the knowledge and ability each role requires.
2. Build role-based training on real tasks, with practice.
3. Assess whether people can perform the new work.
4. Stand up champions, office hours, and quick-reference support.
5. Run support through the adoption phase and log the blockers.
6. Feed recurring blockers back into training and the change plan.

## Anti-patterns

- A generic course for every role.
- Counting attendance as adoption.
- Ending support when training ends.
- Champions chosen for availability, not credibility with peers.
- No channel for the front line to report what is not working.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Role-based training built on real tasks.
- [ ] Capability assessed by performance.
- [ ] Champions and support channels running.
- [ ] Blockers logged and addressed.
- [ ] Support continues through adoption.
- [ ] Recurring blockers fed back into the plan.
