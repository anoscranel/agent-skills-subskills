# Sustaining And Reinforcing Change

## Standard

Most change efforts are abandoned at the reinforcement phase: the launch succeeds, attention moves on, and people drift back to the old way. Reinforcement is the work that prevents reversion — recognising and rewarding the new behaviour, making it the path of least resistance, and removing the old way's supports. Lewin's 'refreeze' is the point: the change is not done until the new way is the normal way.

Embedding makes the change structural rather than dependent on the change team: the new behaviour is written into processes, role expectations, measures, and systems, and leaders keep modelling it after the launch. Adoption is measured over time, not at go-live, and a dip is a signal to reinforce, not to declare failure. The change effort closes only when the new behaviour persists without the change team's support.

Reinforcement also has a budget and an owner. It is scheduled as real work with named responsibility, because the phase that belongs to everyone belongs to no one. Where a dip appears, the cause is diagnosed before more communication is sent — a process that still fights the new behaviour will revert no matter how often the vision is repeated.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Lewin> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADKAR>.

## Decision rules

- Plan reinforcement from the start; do not treat go-live as the end.
- Recognise and reward the new behaviour; make it the easiest path.
- Remove the supports and workarounds of the old way.
- Embed the change in processes, expectations, measures, and systems.
- Measure adoption over time; treat a dip as a reinforcement signal.
- Close only when the new behaviour persists without the change team.

## Procedure

1. Define what sustained adoption looks like and how to measure it.
2. Recognise and reward the new behaviour visibly.
3. Remove old-way workarounds and supports.
4. Embed the new behaviour in process, roles, and measures.
5. Track adoption over time and reinforce on any dip.
6. Confirm the behaviour persists, then close the change effort.

## Anti-patterns

- Declaring success at go-live.
- Moving attention on before reinforcement.
- Leaving old-way workarounds in place.
- Measuring adoption once, at launch.
- Closing while the new behaviour still depends on the change team.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Sustained adoption defined and measured.
- [ ] New behaviour recognised and rewarded.
- [ ] Old-way supports removed.
- [ ] Change embedded in process and measures.
- [ ] Adoption tracked over time with reinforcement on dips.
- [ ] Closure only after persistence confirmed.
