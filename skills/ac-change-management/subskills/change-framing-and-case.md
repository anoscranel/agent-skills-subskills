# Change Framing And Case

## Standard

The case for change answers three questions before any planning: what must change, why it cannot stay as it is, and what happens if nothing changes. Its central claim is that adoption, not delivery, is the deliverable — a system that ships but is not used has produced cost, not value. The change manager distinguishes the change itself from the project that produces it: the project delivers the artefact; the change effort delivers the new behaviour.

The case also names who must change and how. A change framed only at the organisational level hides the individual transitions that decide success: each affected group has a current way of working, a reason it persists, and a cost of switching. Naming those concretely is what turns an abstract initiative into a change plan.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change_management> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizational_change>.

## Decision rules

- State what must change, why the status quo is untenable, and the cost of no change.
- Treat adoption, not delivery, as the deliverable — name the behaviour change required.
- Distinguish the change from the project that produces it; plan both.
- Name each affected group and the specific transition each must make.
- Record the reason the current way persists; it is what the change must overcome.
- A case built only on leadership's rationale, not the affected group's reality, is incomplete.

## Procedure

1. Define the change and the behaviour it requires, in one sentence.
2. Write the case: why now, why this, cost of no change.
3. List the affected groups and the transition each must make.
4. Record why the current way persists and what must overcome it.
5. State the adoption outcome in observable terms.
6. Share the case with a sample of the affected groups and adjust.

## Anti-patterns

- Framing the change as the delivery of a system, not a change in behaviour.
- A case that argues only from leadership's goals.
- Treating adoption as automatic once the artefact ships.
- Ignoring why the current way of working persists.
- No observable definition of what 'adopted' means.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Change and required behaviour stated.
- [ ] Case written with cost of no change.
- [ ] Affected groups and transitions named.
- [ ] Persistence of the current way explained.
- [ ] Adoption outcome observable.
- [ ] Case validated with affected groups.
