# Change Models And Roadmaps

## Standard

Change models give the effort a structure. Lewin's unfreeze-change-refreeze is the oldest: loosen the hold of the current way, move to the new one, then stabilise it. Kotter's eight steps turn that into a leadership sequence — urgency, coalition, vision, communication, empowerment, short-term wins, consolidation, and anchoring. ADKAR works at the individual level: awareness of the need, desire to participate, knowledge of how, ability to perform, and reinforcement to sustain. Each model is a lens, not a recipe, and the honest position is that they scaffold thinking rather than predict outcomes.

The roadmap sequences the work against the chosen model: when the case is made, when the coalition forms, when communication and training land, when adoption is supported, and when reinforcement begins. It is deliberately different from the project plan — it tracks adoption milestones, not delivery milestones, and it extends past go-live into the reinforcement phase where most change efforts are abandoned.

In practice the models are combined: Lewin supplies the broad phases, Kotter the leadership actions within them, and ADKAR the check that each individual has what they need to make the transition. The roadmap records which model governs which decision, so the team is not arguing from different frameworks at once.

Primary sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADKAR> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kotter>.

## Decision rules

- Choose a model as a lens: Lewin for phases, Kotter for leadership sequence, ADKAR for individuals.
- Do not treat a model as a formula that guarantees adoption.
- Sequence the roadmap against the model: case, coalition, communication, training, support, reinforcement.
- Track adoption milestones, not delivery milestones.
- Extend the roadmap past go-live into reinforcement.
- Name the model used and why it fits this change and culture.

## Procedure

1. Select the change model that fits the change and culture.
2. Translate the model into the phases this effort will follow.
3. Sequence the work into a roadmap with adoption milestones.
4. Place communication, training, and support on the roadmap.
5. Schedule the reinforcement phase beyond go-live.
6. Record the model and the roadmap, and review them each phase.

## Anti-patterns

- Applying a model as a checklist that guarantees success.
- A roadmap that ends at go-live.
- Tracking delivery milestones and calling them adoption.
- Choosing a model because it is fashionable, not because it fits.
- No reinforcement phase planned.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Model chosen with rationale.
- [ ] Phases translated to this change.
- [ ] Roadmap carries adoption milestones.
- [ ] Communication, training, support sequenced.
- [ ] Reinforcement phase scheduled.
- [ ] Model and roadmap reviewed each phase.
